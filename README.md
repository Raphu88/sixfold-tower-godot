# Sixfold Tower (Godot prototype)

A first playable test of the new magic system: **bonds** marked on body parts that fill through combat. The design lives in the *Sixfold Tower: Magic Redesign* doc; this prototype tests one question: **does filling bonds through combat feel good?**

## Running it

1. Install [Godot 4.3](https://godotengine.org/download) or newer (the standard version; no .NET needed).
2. In the Godot project manager, choose **Import** and pick this folder's `project.godot`.
3. Press **F5** to play.

Everything visual is drawn in code, so there are no art assets. Sounds and music are recorded CC0 effects in `assets/audio`, and the interface's three fonts (SIL Open Font License) are in `assets/fonts` (see [CREDITS.md](CREDITS.md)).

## Playing on a phone

A web version is built automatically every time `main` changes: **https://raphu88.github.io/sixfold-tower-godot/**. Open it in your phone's browser, turn the phone sideways, and tap the **[ ]** button for fullscreen. It also works in a desktop browser.

On a touchscreen the whole game is drawn bigger so it reads on a small screen (how much is the **Interface size** setting: see Settings below; a phone's Normal is 1.25x what it used to be), and on-screen controls appear as soon as you touch it: a joystick under your left thumb (past a light tilt you walk at full speed; there is no sprint), and buttons on the right for attack (hold to draw a bow), roll, parry, your marks' spells and weapon swap, a **Take** button that appears only while you stand on gear you could swap for, a **Tools** button that appears only while you hold a secret bond (tap for the last tool, hold and slide for the tool ring), an **Item** button at the bottom (it shows the item it will use and how many you have; tap it to use that item, or hold it to open a ring of your belt items (it opens beside your thumb, over the buttons, so nothing hides it), slide your finger toward one and let go to use it; the small arrow beside it picks the next item), and small **Bag** (inventory), **Climber** and **Menu** buttons at the top right. Aiming is automatic: you face the nearest monster in reach, or the way you're moving.

**Controllers.** Plug in (or pair) an Xbox, PlayStation or similar controller and just press a button. The prompts on screen (the controls panel, the gear card's "take it", the card screens, "press ... to begin your next climb") switch to controller buttons as soon as you use it, with PlayStation names on a PlayStation pad, and back to keys the moment you touch the keyboard or mouse (or to touch wording on a phone). Holding LB (L1) turns the face buttons, RB and RT into your six marks' casts; hold the cast button to open the spell ring and point with the right stick (every ring stops time while it's open). The button layout lives in `scripts/controls.gd` (`PAD` and `PAD_CAST`), read by `scripts/pad.gd`.

**If buttons are scrambled in your browser, use Settings & Controls → Controller setup.** Browsers don't all report a controller the same way. Chrome and Edge (and Firefox with many pads) say "standard layout" and everything just works. Firefox often reports an Xbox pad as raw button numbers instead, which differ by system and driver, so the game's buttons land on the wrong ones (or nowhere). The game copes in three ways (`scripts/pad.gd`): a controller the browser or Godot knows passes straight through; an unknown Xbox-like one gets a guessed layout (`FALLBACK`), and the first time you use an unknown controller the Entity mentions the setup; and **Settings & Controls → Controller setup** (`scripts/pad_setup.gd`) walks you through every control one at a time ("Press the button you want for Jump", "Pull the trigger you want for Attack", "Push the stick you want to move with: up"...), records whatever the controller sends (a button, an axis and its direction, a trigger resting at -1, a d-pad sent as buttons, axes or one "hat" axis) and saves it for that controller. Any button starts it (on a controller whose layout is already known, the d-pad moves between the page's buttons and A presses the focused one); once given, your Jump button skips a step and your Roll button goes back one; keys, mouse and touch work too (Esc leaves without saving). **Reset to default** forgets it. Underneath, **What the controller sends** shows the raw button numbers and axis values as they arrive: if something is still off, that's the thing to report. (Godot 4.3's web export also lost the triggers of "standard" pads; `controls.gd` `WEB_STANDARD_FIX` corrects that.)

## Gear: weapons, armor and worn pieces

Gear is everything you carry and wear. Weapons live in `scripts/weapons.gd`; armor, the worn pieces, drops, wear and heirlooms in `scripts/gear.gd`. Names, text and numbers are plain data there, so they're easy to change. Everything is drawn in code, with its own icon.

**Slots.** Gear lasts one climb. You carry **1 fists** (always), **2 a melee weapon** and **3 a ranged weapon**, and you wear a **chest** piece (armor), a **helm**, **gloves**, **legs**, **boots**, a **cloak**, an **amulet**, **two rings** and a **backpack**. An **off-hand** slot is kept for later (it shows locked). Every climb starts with a **sword**, a **shortbow** and nothing else, except where you hold an heirloom (below). The HUD shows only the weapon in your hand (swapping with 1-3, the controller or the phone's Swap button pops up all three weapon slots for a moment); the **inventory** (I) shows everything you wear.

**Gear records.** Each piece is a small record, not just a name: its **base** (which piece it is), its **rarity**, its **traits**, its **rank** (0 to 3, tempered at the bench; 4 with a full vessel), a **rune** (a lasting rune from the bench, or none), for a named piece its **name** (`named`), and, for plain Common pieces, its **wear**. Helpers in `gear.gd` make (`make`, `make_named`, `roll_gear`), convert (`rec`, `code`), describe (`display`, `describe`, `trait_lines`, `stats`), compare (`compare`, `plays_like`) and add up (`total_effects`, `capped_keys`, `protection`) records. Old saves, which kept heirlooms as plain names, are converted automatically when they load.

**Finding gear.** Slain monsters (not beasts) rarely drop a piece (4%), with a rarity by floor (see **Rarities and traits** below), likelier one that suits them: knights favour chain mail, plate, swords, maces, greatswords, iron helms and gauntlets; archers bows, crossbows, leather and leather caps; smiths hammers, maces, armor, gauntlets and hobnail boots; bats dusk cloaks; and so on (`FAVOUR` in `gear.gd`). A drop can be for any slot. Rings and amulets don't appear on the bondless first climb. A Forgotten Cache's urn of essence sometimes holds a piece too (35%), a camp offers a piece of gear as a fourth card beside its three items, and a vault may hold one (Fine at the least); caches, camps and vaults roll one rarity row higher than monsters, and the floor 9 camp's piece is Rare. Gear lies on the floor with its name in its rarity's colour (Fine and better pieces also glint). Walk over it with an empty slot and you take it (a second ring goes on your other hand); otherwise a card names its **slot** and compares it with what you wear there (better numbers in green with an up arrow, worse in red with a down arrow), and **Y** (or **Take** on a phone) swaps: your old piece goes in your **pack** if there's room, else it's dropped where you stand, so you can change your mind. The first time you find a worn piece, the Entity explains the slots.

**The pack.** Besides what you wear you carry a **pack** of **4 spare pieces** (`PACK_SIZE` in `gear.gd`; a Climber's Pack holds 2 more). Swapping gear puts your old piece in it while there's room. The inventory's **Pack** view (beside Worn and Heirlooms) shows it: choose a pack piece to **Wear it** (what you wore takes its place) or **Drop it here**, and the gear at your feet has **Into your pack**. Survive a climb (or read a recall scroll) and your pack comes down to the **train** with you, to sell, keep in your stash, or wear next time (the **Loadout** in your car); fall and it's lost. What you wore stays in the tower when the climb ends, unless you keep it as an heirloom: only the pack comes back down.

**Protection** from everything you wear adds up (plate 35%, an iron helm 6% and chain leggings 5% make 46%) but never goes past **50%** (`PROTECTION_CAP`).

### Rarities and traits: ordinary, enchanted and relics

Gear comes in five **rarities** (`RARITIES` in `gear.gd`), each with its colour on the floor label, the drop's glint, the inventory slot's edge and the card: **Common** (parchment), **Fine** (green, 1 trait, weapons +5% damage), **Rare** (blue, 2 traits, weapons +10%), **Storied** (amber, its own named rule and 1 trait, weapons +12%) and **Regarded** (gold, a beast's own weapon or armour: its own named rule and **4 traits**, weapons +15%; see **Regarded pieces** below). Rings and amulets are Fine by nature, so they carry one trait fewer. Backpacks take no traits. Only plain **Common** pieces wear; anything with a trait, and anything finer, never does.

**Ordinary or enchanted** (the owner's direction: "keep tiers but make each piece exciting; some not enchanted and some enchanted"). A **Common** piece is **ordinary**: not enchanted, no traits, only its own use, and every base piece has one clear use of its own (no two the same; the cards say "ordinary"). **Fine** and **Rare** pieces are **enchanted**: their traits, and their card's first line says "Enchanted: <essence> essence" in that essence's colour (the essence of its first trait). Rings and amulets are enchanted by nature, each in its own essence (`PIECES` "kind"); the Climber's Pack and the Bandolier are Rare by nature but ordinary. **Storied** and **Regarded** pieces are **relics**: a name, their own rule, and a card line saying whether they're enchanted (the Lastbell Revolver isn't). Helpers in `gear.gd`: `enchanted`, `enchant_kind`, `shot_kind`. **Relics' histories:** every named piece has a `history` in `NAMED`, blank for the owner to write (in `gear.gd`, or the Data editor's Words → Gear → Named pieces); a card shows it only once it isn't blank.

**No trait twice.** A piece never carries the same trait twice, whatever made it: a drop, a cache, a camp, a vault, today's piece, a Proving relic, Join or Retrait at the bench. `gear.gd make()` (which every piece passes through, old saves too) replaces a repeat with another trait that fits the slot and isn't on the piece, the same one every time, so a piece in an old save is cleaned when it loads; if no trait is left for the slot, the repeat goes. Every slot but the backpack has at least four traits, so a Regarded piece always has four different ones.

**Hunter's Keen Eyes** outline gear on the floor in its rarity's colour, even through the dark.

**Wear.** Only **Common** pieces wear, and pieces with traits never will. Each use takes a little condition off the pieces it uses: a landed swing wears your melee weapon and gloves, a shot your bow or crossbow, a blow that lands on you your chest, helm, legs and cloak, a roll or jump your boots (`WEAR` and `WEAR_BY` in `gear.gd`; a piece used all climb long gets badly worn near the end of it). At **25%** a piece is **badly worn**: its bonus is halved (a chest piece protects half as much; a weapon hits 25% softer), it shows cracks on its icon and its card says so. At **0** it **breaks** and its slot is empty: a broken weapon leaves you your fists until you find another. Each piece's condition shows as a thin bar under it in the inventory. Heirlooms start every climb in full repair.

**Melee weapons** are combo chains; every chain ends in a heavy finisher. Damage is a multiple of the base strike (8), as with the sword.

**Pistols** (`kind: "gun"` in `weapons.gd`) go in the ranged slot. An ordinary pistol starts reloading by itself once it's empty; reloading is something you do, so **a roll breaks it off** (it starts again from the beginning after the roll), and so does putting the pistol away. You walk a little slower while reloading (`reload_move`), and the quick-draw traits and Archer's Bracers reload pistols faster. Lead shot that lands fills your hand's mark as arrows and bolts do. The HUD's weapon slot shades while it reloads and shows a pip for each shot (lit while loaded); on a phone the attack button reads "Revolver 4/6" and its ring fills as it reloads. The sounds are the bank's own, pitched: thunder and a crossbow's clack for the flintlock, a quick clack and a ring of steel for the revolver, powder poured and a ramrod's click, a ratchet and a lock's turn for reloading.

**Essence shot** (`ESSENCE_SHOT` in `weapons.gd`). An **enchanted** pistol (Fine or Rare, or an enchanted relic) fires its enchantment's essence instead of lead, and **never stops to reload**: each empty chamber fills itself after a moment (`refill`: 1.8 s for a flintlock, 0.5 s a chamber for a revolver), in hand or holstered, even through a roll. An essence shot doesn't fill your hand's mark (the essence is the pistol's, not yours). What it does follows the essence:

| Essence | Shot |
| --- | --- |

| Iron | pierces every foe in its path |
| Air | flies 70% faster and half again as far; its chambers fill 30% sooner |
| Fire | bursts in flame over the foes around (60% of its damage) |
| Water | chills what it hits, slowing it for 2 s |

**Heirlooms.** Survive a climb (beat its beast, or reach the Regard on the first climb without falling) and, after the victory screen, you choose **one** piece you wore to keep as an **heirloom**: a card for each slot you fill (any piece already kept is left out), and one to keep nothing new. Your **rings** share one card and are kept **together**. With many cards they sit in two rows. Every later climb starts with your heirlooms in their slots, in full repair. Each slot holds one heirloom (Rings holds both rings); keeping another piece for the same slot replaces it. The heirloom slots are **melee**, **ranged**, **chest**, **helm**, **gloves**, **legs**, **boots**, **cloak**, **amulet**, **rings** and **backpack**, with **off-hand** kept for later. There's one free heirloom per survived climb. The **inventory**'s Heirlooms view shows them on the same body layout, and the climber screen's Record tab lists them. Starting a new climber forgets them. Slots and text live in `HEIRLOOM_SLOTS` and `HEIRLOOM_TEXT` in `scripts/gear.gd`; heirlooms are saved with your profile as records.

The dev tools have a **Gear** row: a button for every weapon, armor and worn piece, and one to take off every piece, to try them at once; a **Finds** row drops a Fine, Rare or Storied find (or any named piece) at your feet, and an **Items** row puts any item on your belt.

## Items and camps

**Items** (`scripts/items.gd`, where their names, text and numbers live) are carried on your **belt** through the climb. The belt holds **4 kinds** of item, up to **3 of each** (a Satchel or Climber's Pack: 4 of each; a Belt Pouch or Climber's Pack: a fifth kind; the recall scroll is always one). Walk onto a new kind with a full belt and the **take card** opens (the monsters wait): swap one stack out (it's left where you stand) or leave the new item on the floor; either way it waits until you step off and back on. A camp's or vault's item with a full belt is left at your feet. **G** (or the Item button) uses the selected item and **T** (or the small arrow) picks the next; **hold** G, the Item button, X or the d-pad down to open the **item ring**: time stops, point at an item (the mouse, a stick, or slide your finger from the button) and let go to use it. A quick tap never opens it. The HUD shows the selected item beside the weapon, with its count; the inventory (I) shows the belt, with an empty cell for each kind it has room for. An item that would do nothing (a draught at full health, a vial with full marks) isn't used up.

**Staples and the rest** (the owner's direction: "health and stamina potions are common and cheap, a staple for any climber; other consumables are a little more unique"). The **Healing Draught** and **Stamina Draught** are staples (`staple` in `items.gd`): every climb starts with one of each (`START`), they cost 3 at the store (`SUPPLY_PRICE`, half their small worth), and they're most of what slain monsters leave. Everything else is rarer: it costs its full worth (10 to 20), monsters drop it far less often, and camps and vaults offer only these rarer things, never a staple. Slain monsters (not beasts) sometimes leave an item (10%); walk over it to pick it up. Warding Oil, Stoneskin Draughts and the two items of the secret essences (Heartroot and Ash of Unmaking) are never dropped by monsters; camps and vaults offer them. Every item is also on the train's store shelf and brewed at the bench. Items that work for a while (Antivenom, Heartroot, Briar Tonic, Wardbreaker, Lantern Oil, the Scroll of Seeing) add to the same numbers your gear does (`buff` in `items.gd`); using another refreshes it.

**The belt at the end of a climb.** Survive a climb (beat its beast, reach the Regard without falling, or read a recall scroll) and whatever is left on your belt breaks into **essence crystals** at half its worth, one crystal per essence (`value` and `BREAK_SHARE` in `items.gd`); the victory screen says what it became. Fall and it's lost.

**Bonds and items:**

- **Ashwalker:** Smoke Bombs last twice as long.
- **Flameblade:** Edge Oil builds heat twice as fast.
- **Flame Contractor:** Fire Flasks burst 50% wider (and a foe they kill leaves Kindled ground, as with your other fire).
- **Hunter:** Keen Eyes outline dropped gear in its rarity's colour; Flare Powder lasts twice as long.

**Camps.** On a full climb, the stairs of floors 3 and 9 lead first to a **camp** (`CAMP_AFTER` in `items.gd`): the open room with a campfire and no monsters. You rest fully (all health and stamina, burns and poison healed), then choose one of four cards (keys 1 to 4, the arrows and Enter, the d-pad and A, click, or tap): three items, or a piece of gear (taking it leaves your old piece by the fire). Then take the stair on to the next floor (a camp isn't a floor: after floor 3's camp comes floor 4). Each camp comes once a climb; the bondless first climb has none.

- **Surgeon's Steady Hands:** healing items heal 30% more and work faster.
- **Rune Artist's Runesmith:** after taking an item at a camp, a Rune Artist also chooses a rune for their gear (Sharpness, Warding or Swiftness, as with Inscribe) that lasts the rest of the climb, the beast included, or crafts one more item. Casting Inscribe still changes the rune for one floor.

## The train (between climbs)

After the Regard, every climb ends on **the train**, a steampunk train waiting outside the towers, whether you won, fell or read a recall scroll, and every climb starts from it (the first climb is bondless and has no train: it appears with the Regard). **The train's cars are the menu:** you see the inside of one car at a time, filling the screen, and move along the train from car to car. Everything is drawn in code: wood panelling and buttoned velvet, brass rails and rivets, wall lamps that glow, arched windows on the dusk with the six towers on the horizon (each with a window lit in its essence's colour), and each car's furniture holding its things, which you tap (or focus and press) directly. The words, prices and numbers live in `scripts/train.gd`.

**Moving along: the rail.** On the train the menu's **rail** (`scripts/rail.gd`, see Menus below) is always down the left, and it is the train's only menu, in the owner's order from top to bottom: **who you are** (your name and your six marks; it's a button: click, tap or press it and the climber screen opens beside the rail, and it's the first stop for the keys and the controller), then the six cars front to back, the **Engine**, the **Outfitter's Car**, **Your Car**, the **Workshop**, the **Library** and the **Settings & Controls Car** (each with its own icon drawn in code: a locomotive, a pair of scales, a bed, an anvil and hammer, a bound book, a signal lever; and on a computer a line under it), then under a thin rule **Dev Tools** (a page beside the rail, not a car), and at its foot **Exit to Title Station** and, on a computer, **Quit game** (a browser or a phone can't quit a game, so there it's left out: Exit to Title Station is the way out). **Inventory** isn't on the rail any more: it's the Inventory button in Your Car, or **I**. **Start over** isn't either: it's in the Settings & Controls Car, set apart as the emergency brake (see below). The cars fill the rest of the screen, and the train opens on your car. Choose a car on the rail and the train slides there; swipe left or right on a phone (the train follows your finger and settles on the nearest car; a quick flick goes on to the next), drag it or turn the mouse wheel on a computer, or press **Q** / **E** (**LB** / **RB**): the rail's gilt light always follows the car you're in, and moves while you swipe. Choose the climber screen or Dev Tools and it opens beside the same rail (the rail never changes; a dim gilt mark keeps your car); **Close**, **Esc**, **B** or choosing a car returns to the car you were on. **Keys and controller:** the train starts with the focus on the rail; **Up** / **Down** move along it, sliding to each car or showing each page as you pass; **Right**, **Enter** or **A** goes into the car (onto its starting point); **Left** past the first thing in a car, or **Esc** / **B**, comes back to the rail on that car. On the rail Esc and B do nothing more (only the engine's lever leaves the train). On a phone the rail narrows to icons with short names (**Engine**, **Outfitter**, **Your Car**, **Workshop**, **Library**, **Settings**, **Dev**, **Station**), and the cars' rows share the height so everything fits an 800 x 400 screen without scrolling. Your **crystals** (a gem and how much essence you hold of each kind) run along the bottom of the cars on a brass strip. A choice's card (its stats set against yours, the price as essence gems, "needs N more X", and its buttons) is a brass-framed placard in the car, beside the furniture. The Entity's tips appear at the top of each car the first time you're there.

**The train on end (a mock-up in Dev Tools).** Dev Tools has **Train rail: Menu / Train on end** (Menu at first; kept with the settings). Train on end draws the same rail, with the same items, order and behaviour, as the train itself standing on end: the station's sign at the top (your name and marks: still the button for the climber screen), the engine under it with its headlamp, red cowcatcher, boiler, smokestack, dome, driving wheels and cab, then each car below it in its room's colour (lit arched windows, a gilt-lined panel, its icon and name on its side, wheels on a track down the left edge), joined by couplings, and under the last car a platform whose enamel signs are Dev Tools, Exit to Title Station and Quit game (on a phone, side by side). The car you're in is ringed in gilt and its windows glow brighter. It fits a computer's screen well; on a phone (800 x 400) it fits too, but the cars are short and stubby.

Its **destination board** has a plaque for each tower (its essence and your best floor there; the last one you climbed is chosen at first). **Money.** Essence crystals are the only currency, and you hold **one crystal of each kind**, which grows as you add to it (old saves' piles combine as they load). They come from:

- **Condensing at every stair.** A share of the essence you bank (**20%** at first, `CONDENSE_DEFAULT` in `train.gd`) condenses into crystals instead of soaking into you: per kind, in proportion, whole essence only (a kind with too little for one whole essence is banked in full). Only the rest is banked, so only the rest grows your marks, settles as density, feeds a secret bond or pools raw in an Unbound's parts. It works from the very first climb, so a new climber has money at the first train. The stair's banner says it: "Banked 46 essence · 11 condensed into crystals". **Settings → To crystals** changes the share (0%, 10%, 20%, 35% or 50%: more money, slower growth, or the other way round). It's saved with the settings, not the climber, so a new climber keeps it.
- **The outfitter's pay for every climb**, however it ends (a fall too, the summit, a recall scroll, the first climb's Regard): crystals of the tower's kind, **2 for every floor reached** and **3 more for every floor past your best in that tower** before the climb (`CLIMB_PAY`). The fall screen, the victory or recall screen and the Regard say it in gold ("The outfitter pays for your climb: +30 Water"), and his greeting in the store car mentions it until you depart. A fall on floor 6 pays 12, or 30 if it's a new best; a summit pays 24, or up to 60 the first time.
- Selling and breaking down, vault and secret-room prizes, champions and mirror images, a recall scroll (your unbanked essence) and the belt at the end of a survived climb.

Every price is in one kind of essence (a consumable's own kind; a piece's trait or named kind, else its slot's: chest and helm Stone, gloves and melee Blade, legs, ranged and backpack Iron, boots and cloak Air, amulets Fire, rings Water), shown as a gem and a number, red when you can't pay; the card says why in words ("needs 12 more Fire"). The one exception is the recall scroll, which takes any kind. Prices: a piece's `BASE_PRICE` (8 to 20) times its rarity's `RARITY_PRICE`: **Common x0.5** (half what they first were, 4 to 10), Fine x2, Rare x4, Storied x8, Regarded x14 (so a Fine piece costs 4x its Common, the finds you sell keep their worth, and a beast's own piece sells for the most). **The staples** (Healing Draught 3 Stone, Stamina Draught 3 Iron: `SUPPLY_PRICE`) cost half their worth and come first on the shelf; everything else costs its worth in `items.gd` (10 to 20). Selling gives 40% of the price (at least 1), so a Common sells for 2 to 4. In the **Inventory**, the Essence crystals row shows how much each kind holds; **Consume 10** banks up to 10 of it into you at a time (`CRYSTAL["step"]` in `items.gd`). The Entity explains the train, the store, the chest, the bench and enchanting the first time you see each. The stash (`chest` in the save), pack, the Loadout (`wear`), packed supplies, the store's stock and the last climb's pay are saved with your profile (the `train` section; old saves load with an empty stash and nothing chosen to wear).

**What a climb earns** (rough, at 20%): a floor gives about 30 essence low down and 60 by floor 5, so a fall on floor 6 banks about 220, condenses about 44 (most of it the tower's kind) and is paid 12 (30 on a new best): roughly 40 to 60 crystal essence, enough for a few Common pieces and supplies in the tower's kind. A summit gathers about 800, condenses about 160 and is paid 24 or more (these are estimates from the monster budgets in `floors.gd`, before caches, puzzles and vaults).

**Falling, and lost things.** Fall in a tower (after the Regard; the bondless first climb keeps its own rules, and ends in the Regard) and the fall screen shows for a moment (what you lost, and "Back to the train in 3"), then you're back on the train, on your own car, ready for the next climb: on its own after 3 seconds (`FALL_WAIT` in `main.gd`), or at once on a tap, Enter, Space, a click, R or the controller's A or View. Your **pack** and your **belt** are lost (the belt's items don't break into crystals on a fall), and there's a **35%** chance the **weapon in your hand** goes too (your melee weapon if your fists were out; never the starting sword or bow). **Heirlooms are never lost**: an heirloom in your hand stays yours. The fall screen says plainly what went ("Lost on the way down: your pack (3 pieces), your belt, your Coral Trident"). The tower remembers the **last 6 pieces** you lost, with the tower and floor where they fell (saved with your profile; old saves load with nothing lost). On a later climb of that tower, each floor of rooms within **2 floors** of where things fell has a **25%** chance of a **lost cache**: your own fallen pack with a broken strap and a blade poking out, under a faint pale light, in an ordinary room (never a puzzle hall, a sealed wing or a trap room, and traps keep clear of it). Walk onto it and up to **3** of your lost pieces come back, into your pack while there's room, else at your feet; found pieces are no longer lost. Belt items never come back. The Entity explains the first fall that loses things and the first lost cache. The numbers and words live in `scripts/lost.gd` (`WEAPON_CHANCE`, `HEIRLOOM_WEAPON`, `KEEP`, `CACHE_CHANCE`, `NEAR`, `CACHE_MAX`, `TEXT`).

## Stations, renown, titles and boons

Between towers the train can **stop at a station** instead of climbing. Nothing you carry is at risk there: it's where you train, fight in the tournament, take on challenges and spend your **renown**. The names are plain placeholders (the station is "First Station"); every name, number and word is in the Data editor (Words, Numbers and Colours: **Stations, renown, titles and boons**), and more stations can be added to `STATIONS` in `scripts/stations.gd` (each lists the stops it has).

**Getting there and back.** In the **Engine**, the destination board has a sign in its corner: **Stations** turns the board to the stations (and **Towers** turns it back). Choose the station's plaque; its placard says what's there and your renown, and the **lever** takes the train there. At the station the rail down the left lists its stops, in the same left-rail style as the train: who you are (still the climber screen), **Training Gym**, **Tournament Arena**, **Challenges**, **Renown Board**, **Boon Shrine**, and at the foot **Back to the train** (it goes back to the Engine). The station is drawn behind its page in code: a covered platform at dusk, the six towers on the horizon, iron columns, a scalloped canopy, glowing lamps, the station's sign, and on the walls something of the stop you're at (the gym's rings and weights, the arena's banners, the challenges' pennants, the board's pinned notices, the shrine's candles). **Controls:** on the rail Up / Down move between stops (each shows as you pass), Right, Enter or A goes into the stop; **Q / E** (LB / RB) move to the next stop from anywhere on the page; Esc or B goes back from a card to its tile, from the page to the rail, and from the rail back to the train. Every stop is a list of tiles on the left and a card on the right: hovering or moving the focus onto a tile shows its card, pressing it (a click, Enter, A, a tap) does too and puts the keys on the card's button. On a phone or at a big Interface size the card goes under the list and its button comes up right under its name; the page scrolls.

**Renown** (`scripts/renown.gd`, kept with each climber; an old save loads with none) only grows. A climb earns **1 for every floor reached** and **2 for every floor past your best in that tower**, however it ends (`CLIMB`), and **20 for a beast felled**; drills, bouts, challenges and deeds earn more. Anything later can add to it with one hook, `Renown.earn(profile, amount, reason)` (the reason is what the renown board lists). **Ranks** come at thresholds (`RANKS`): Unknown 0, Noticed 25, Known 60, Respected 120, Famed 200, Renowned 320, Celebrated 480. A rank unlocks drills, tournament tiers, challenges, boons and the outfitter's upgrades. A gauge at the top of every stop shows your renown, rank and how far to the next.

**Training Gym:** drills, each with a clear goal and a medal (bronze, silver, gold), run in a small arena of a tower (`scripts/arena.gd`; a line at the top of the screen says how it's going and the time left):
- **Parry the Glints** (40 s, two soft-hitting knights): parry as many blows as you can (4 / 7 / 10).
- **Five Down**: fell five foes as fast as you can (32 / 22 / 15 seconds).
- **Roll Gauntlet** (30 s among archers; Noticed): the fewer hits, the better (6 / 3 / 1).
- **Post to Post** (Noticed): touch six posts in order, as fast as you can (30 / 21 / 15 seconds); an arrow at the screen's edge points to the next post.
- **Through the Pack** (Known): hit as many of six dummies as you can with one cast (2 / 3 / 5).
A drill's first bronze, silver and gold each earn renown (`renown`: a gold the first time earns all three). A fall in a drill doesn't fell you: the drill ends with no medal.

**Tournament Arena:** tiers of bouts (`TIERS`), each tier a ladder: win a bout to open the next. A bout is waves of tower monsters, champions, your own **mirror image** (`mirror.gd`) or a beast's shadow, with **rules** (no healing, stamina halved, fierce foes). Tiers unlock with rank and get harder: **Copper Ladder** (Kraken's monsters, fee 5), **Iron Ladder** (Known; the Wyvern's, fee 10) and **Gilt Ladder** (Famed; the Chimera's, fee 15). Each bout costs an **entry fee** in crystals of any kind (the biggest first); losing costs nothing more. A win pays renown and crystals of the tier's tower's kind (a repeat win, a fifth of the renown and half the crystals: `REPEAT`); winning a tier's last bout wins the tier and gives a **relic** the first time (a named piece you don't have, into your stash; later wins now and then: `RELIC_CHANCE`). A card lists the foes wave by wave, the rules, the fee and what a win pays.

**Challenges:** harder one-off events with a twist (`CHALLENGES`), unlocked by rank, no fee, paid out the first time you win each (renown and crystals; the last one a relic too): **One Hit** (any hit fells you), **Silenced** (no spells or techniques), **Two Reflections** (two mirror images at once), **Against the Clock** (three waves in 60 seconds) and **The Beast's Shadow**.

In the arena a fall never ends anything: you stay standing and the event is lost. The pause menu's **Give up, back to the station** ends it at once. Nothing done in the arena is kept but the result (no drops, essence or record), and going back writes the result to your save, then the train starts afresh with the station's page open on that stop and the result on its card.

**Renown Board:** your renown and rank, your **recent deeds** (the last 10), the ranks, and the **titles**. Titles (`TITLES`) are earned by deeds: stopping at a station, clearing a drill, felling a beast, 50 floors climbed, 3 climbs in a row without falling, a gold in every drill, each tournament tier won, beating your mirror image, winning a challenge or every challenge, reaching Renowned. Choose an earned title and **Wear this title**: it shows under your name on the rail (in the italic face) and in your book (the line under your name); **Take it off** takes it off. Titles not earned yet show the deed that earns them.

**Boon Shrine:** lasting boons (`scripts/boons.gd`), unlocked by rank and bought with crystals of one kind, a level at a time (each level costs more). They're kept with the climber and go with them on every climb: **Vigor** (+10 health a level), **Deep Breath** (+15 stamina), **Satchel** (an extra Healing Draught on your belt each climb), **Fleetfoot** (+4% speed), **Whetstone** (+6% melee damage), **Haggler's Charm** (the outfitter charges 5% less), **Wide Belt** (one more kind of item on the belt) and **Keen Eye** (on each climb's first floor a finer piece of gear waits near the start). A boon's numbers go on the climber at once (`player.gd boon_fx`, added to what your gear adds).

**The outfitter's upgrades** (`UPGRADES` in `scripts/train.gd`): ranks unlock them for good. **Noticed:** a wider rack (10 pieces). **Known:** a scroll kept aside (the recall scroll he always stocks costs 40 of any kind, not 60). **Respected:** better stock (rack pieces may come Fine; today's piece is likelier Rare, now and then Storied). **Famed:** a regular's discount (10% off everything he sells, on top of Haggler's Charm). **Renowned:** a **special order**: choose a piece on the rack or in the case and **Hold it for me**; it leaves the rack and waits in the **Special order** slot beside today's piece until you buy it, on any later visit (one at a time). The Outfitter's Car has an **Upgrades** sign under the outfitter (on a computer with a line naming the next upgrade and the rank that unlocks it); its card lists every upgrade and what unlocks it.

## Essence density (attributes)

Each essence type is an attribute: Air is Agility, Blade Strength, Stone Vitality, Iron Will, Fire Intellect and Water Spirit. Every bit of essence you bank at a stair (after the share that condenses into crystals) settles in you as **density**, by type and in the body parts that earned it (split by what each part did that floor), and it's kept between climbs. Each type gives a small passive bonus that grows with diminishing returns up to 12%: Air move speed, Blade strike damage, Stone health, Iron stamina recovery, Fire spell power, Water how fast your marks fill. The **climber screen** (Tab) shows your six body parts with their marks, and its Essence tab your density and bonus for every type and the essence settled in each part.

## The secret bonds: Developer, Designer and Manipulator

Three secret bonds, one from each Maker (`scripts/secret_bonds.gd`, where every word and number lives; `scripts/secret_tools.gd` runs the ring and the depth 1 and 2 tools in play; `scripts/secret_deep.gd` the depth 3 to 5 tools; `scripts/secret_page.gd` is the climber screen's Core tab). They're **dev tools for their theme**: the Developer works on your bonds, the Designer on the tower, the Manipulator on the creatures. They go much farther than any tower bond and share no feature with any other bond, item or essence. **A climber holds only one.** It's the **core mark**, kept in the profile as `core_mark` ("", "developer", "designer" or "manipulator") and `core_depth` (0 to 5); the Makers (`makers.gd`, built alongside) grant it, and the dev tools can too. Each depth unlocks one tool, from looking to rewriting: **inspect, switch, edit, reroll, build**, all five built.

**The tool ring.** Hold **B** (**LB + LT** on a controller, the **Tools** button that appears on a phone while you hold a secret bond) and past a quarter second a ring of your unlocked tools opens and time stops; point at one (the mouse, the right stick, or slide your finger from the button) and let go to use it. A tap uses the last tool again, or closes an open panel. Under each tool its **cost shows as gems**: your path's secret essence and ordinary essence. A tool that aims works on what you were aiming at when the ring opened (it's outlined while the ring is open). While a tool works, or its panel is open, a faint **wireframe** of the workings shows in its Maker's colour: the tile grid, trap lanes and door hinges (Designer), monster outlines (Manipulator), your spells' reach (Developer).

**Every tool costs essence, paid like a spell from what you carry** (unbanked): a few units of secret essence from the flow you carry, plus some ordinary essence taken from every kind you carry in proportion. Each unit of secret essence you don't carry (no path, the other path, or none carried) costs **3 essence** instead. The Manipulator's tools cost mostly Life, the Designer's mostly Unmaking, the Developer's mostly ordinary essence plus one unit of your path's. Looking is cheap, rewriting expensive. A tool you can't pay for says what's missing, and nothing is paid for a tool that can't work (no target, a great foe, a locked door). An **Inspect** is paid once and lasts the floor: using it again opens or closes its panel for free.

**The bribe.** On the train, the Developer's Core tab offers the Maker of Marks 120 crystal essence, a Regarded piece of gear from your chest or pack, or a full vessel from your chest (see **The vessel**). **The Core tab.** On the climber screen, a fifth tab appears with a secret bond: its name and depth, every tool (depths still to come shown as such) with what it does and its cost, and its growth. The Developer's also lists every spell's real numbers (on the train; in a climb once Inspect is paid on the floor), the Tune and Edit buttons (on the train), moving marks (depth 5), the bribe and the change of path. The Entity explains the ring and each tool the first time (the Archive's "Secret bond tools").

## Menus and the dev tool

The game opens on a **title screen** that covers the whole game: a tower drawn in code against a night sky, with lit windows, a moon, mist and drifting motes in the six essence colours (`scripts/title.gd`; the name, tagline and footer text are in its `TEXT` list). Its buttons are **Continue** (the climber played last; **Begin your first climb** for a new climber), **Climbers** (the climber picker, naming the current climber: see Climbers and saves below; it appears once there is a climber saved, so a brand-new player sees only Begin your first climb), **Settings**, **Dev tools** and **Start over** (only once you have marks to lose), with a line showing your marks and best floor. The climber screen isn't on the title: once you've chosen a climber it's in the pause menu (and Tab, the Climber button on a phone, or Your car on the train). Arrow keys and Enter, a controller (d-pad or left stick to move, A to press), the mouse, or a tap all work; Esc does nothing there, and the list wraps round. The focused button has a gold outline (the mouse moves it), and the first one is focused when a page opens. Pages opened from the title show over its backdrop and **Back** returns to it. Leaving the title takes you to the tower choice (after the Regard, to the train).

During a climb there are three screens, and each pauses the game while it's open (menus fade in and out quickly):

- **Pause menu:** **Esc** or **P** (**Start** on a controller, **Menu** on a phone). The in-game pages share a **rail** down the left of the screen (`scripts/rail.gd`): at its top who you are (your name, the tower and floor or "On the train", and your six marks as small medallions; it's a button that opens the climber screen, **Tab** shown beside it), then **Resume**, **Inventory**, the **Library** (the Entity's Archive), **Settings & Controls** and **Dev Tools**, and at its foot **Exit to Title** (to play another climber, Exit to Title, then Climbers). The names follow the train's: in a climb the Library and Settings & Controls are single pages beside the rail rather than cars. The page shows to the right of it. The pause page itself sums up the climb (the tower, the floor, the essence you carry unbanked and your marks) with **Restart this climb**, and on a computer **Quit game** (in a browser or on a phone Exit takes you to the title instead). **Settings & Controls** has two tabs, Settings and Controls (click them, or **Q** / **E**, **LB** / **RB**), and under a rule at its foot **Start over this climber** (this climber only; it asks first). **Keys and controller:** the menu opens on Resume; **Up** / **Down** move along the rail and show each page as you pass it; **Right**, **Enter** or **A** goes into the page; **Left** from the page comes back to that page's own item; **Esc** or **B** comes back to the rail, and from the rail closes the menu. A page opened by its own key (Tab, I) closes with Esc at once. **Mouse:** hovering moves the focus (without opening the page), a click opens it, and a **right-click** goes back like Esc. **Touch:** tap a rail item; every page has **Close** at its top right. On a phone the rail narrows to icons with short names. On the train the rail stays on screen the whole time and lists the cars (see The train). The words and icons live in `rail.gd` (`CLIMBER`, `ITEMS`, `TRAIN_CARS`, `TRAIN_PAGES`, `FOOT`, `TRAIN_FOOT`, `FOOT_QUIT`, `TEXT`); the pages' icons are drawn in `ui.gd` `draw_icon`, the cars' and the new ones (book, lever, station lamp) in `rail.gd` `car_icon`.
- **Climber screen:** **Tab** (a short press of **View / Share**, the **Climber** button on a phone, your name at the top of the rail, or the book in Your Car on the train). It's **your book** (`scripts/book.gd`, see **The tree, the library and the book**), in five sections, each a ribbon (turn the pages with the ribbons, **Q** / **E**, **LB** / **RB** on a controller, or a swipe across the page on a phone; the book reopens on the section you used last): **Abilities** (see below), **Tree** (your body map: how you can grow, and the choices waiting), **Marks** (the climber drawn in full, in your chosen look, with **Appearance** under it, and each mark glowing on its body part in its bond's colour and a thin ring of the essence settled there, beside a card for each body part with its mark's medallion, step and growth), **Essence** (density and attribute bonus for each type, the essence settled in each body part, and what you carry unbanked), **Record** (the Entity's record of you: this climb's tower and floor, your counters, each tower's best floor and your heirlooms). Everything the Entity has told you is in the **Library** (see below).
- **Abilities** (`scripts/abilities.gd`): what you can cast right now, and how. It's the climber screen's first tab, so Tab (or the Climber button) shows it, and **holding** Tab or the Climber button a moment opens it straight on Abilities (a peek): let go and you're back in the game, on the tab you had before. From anywhere, `menu.open("abilities")` opens it (the train's Climber page, your car's Climber, and the Techniques page's **All your abilities** button reach it on the train). A marked climber sees each mark (its bond, body part, essence and step, the button that casts it, "hold for the ring" once it has two spells, and on a computer its Body and Passive in a line) with its spells under it: each a card with its medallion, name, what it does in one plain line, the button to press (the key, "LB + X" on a controller, or "tap" / "hold, slide" on a phone, following what you used last; the chosen spell is "on the button") and its cost; spells not learned yet are dimmed with a lock and "learned at step N". The Unbound see their six techniques, one per mark button: name, part, shape and essences, what it does, the button, and "full at 20 · +14 strain". Press a card (Enter, A, a click or a tap) for **more**: the full numbers (a spell's cost, wind-up and the rest and what later steps change, and how its mark fills; a technique's fill, strain, boost, what each essence adds, its shape's numbers and its part's level). On the train the page also has **Try your abilities** (the practice room) and, for the Unbound, **Change techniques**; for a marked climber, **Every mark's steps** goes to the Marks tab. For the Unbound the Marks tab is called **Body**. Every word is in its `TEXT`.
- **The practice room** (`scripts/practice.gd`, the dummies in `scripts/dummy.gd`): **Try your abilities** on the train drops you into a quiet room of the last tower with three training dummies: straw on a post with a painted target. They take every hit (and burns, slows, staggers and shoves), show the damage, never move or strike, and never fall (their health comes back a moment after the last hit). Your marks and techniques start full and refill a moment after each cast; strain still builds, so the Unbound can feel it, but nothing there can fell you. Nothing done there is kept (the save is left alone). A line at the foot of the screen says how to leave: the pause menu's **Back to the train** (or a restart) goes back to the train, on the Abilities page.
- **A mark's page:** choose a card on the Marks tab (click or tap it, or focus it and press **Enter** or **A**). It shows the mark's emblem, its body part and essence, its step and growth (and the banked essence each step needs, or the essence a faint mark still owes), its **Body** and **Passive** effects, and **every spell**: the step that teaches it, how to cast it, its cost, wind-up and other numbers, what it does and what each later step changes. Spells not learned yet are dimmed with a lock and "learned at step N". The steps are listed at the bottom with what each brings. An unmarked part's page says what soaks into it and, where your next mark is forming, which bond it would be. **Q** / **E** (**LB** / **RB**, or the buttons either side of Back) show the next mark; **Back** (**Esc**, **B**) returns to the cards, and **Tab** closes the screen. **Up** / **down** scroll the page.
- **Inventory:** **I** (**d-pad up**, the **Bag** button on a phone). A **paper doll**: what you wear, laid out like a body (helm, amulet, cloak / melee weapon, chest, ranged weapon / ring, gloves, ring / backpack, legs, boots), with fists, the off-hand (locked, for later), gear lying at your feet (marked "new") and a camp's rune beside it, and your belt below: its items with their counts (the quick item gilded) and an empty cell for each kind it still has room for. **Worn / Pack / Heirlooms** at the top switches the doll to your pack's spare pieces or your heirlooms. On the train, I opens your car instead. The weapon in hand is gilded, empty slots show their name, Common pieces show their condition as a thin bar, finer pieces are edged in their rarity's colour, and a piece that is also your heirloom has a small gold mark. Hover over or focus a slot to see its card on the right; press it (**Enter**, **A**, a click or a tap) and, with the keys or a controller, the focus moves onto the card's first button (**B**, **Esc** or **Left** comes back to the slot; the train's cards work the same way): what it does, its traits (each in its essence's colour), its rarity and condition, and its numbers set against the gear at your feet or what every climb starts with (green up arrows where it's better, red down arrows where it's worse), with a "plays like" line for weapons ("Compared with yours: hits harder, slower"); the gear at your feet against what you wear, with **Take it** (or, with two rings on, a button to swap either ring); or an item's text with **Use** (or press the item's slot a second time: the first press shows it, with every input) and **Make it your quick item**.

**Every menu moves the same way** (`scripts/nav.gd`): the mouse moves the focus as it passes over a button; the arrow keys, the d-pad and the left stick are one and the same, a step per press, and holding one repeats at the same steady rate whatever you hold; **Enter**, **A**, a click or a tap presses; **Esc**, **B** or a right-click goes back one level and puts the focus back on what opened the page (in a text box they only leave the box); **Q** / **E** (**LB** / **RB**) switch tabs, the inventory's Worn / Pack / Heirlooms included, and the focus moves onto the new tab; the **mouse wheel** and the **right stick** scroll (the settings' sliders never take the wheel); single lists (the rail, the title, the appearance rows, the card screens) wrap round, while grids and long pages stop at their edges; the Start over question starts on **Cancel**; and the prompts follow what you used last, even halfway through a page ("Press again" or, on a phone, "Tap again"); picking up another device never costs a press: its first button, key, click or tap does its job, and the page's words change once it's let go. On a phone nothing shows a focus outline.

The same key closes its screen (Tab closes the climber screen, I the inventory), and **Esc** or **B** (Circle) goes back a page, or out of the menu from the pause menu or a screen opened by its key; every page shows its Back or Close button with the keys that do the same. **Settings** has music and sound volume, screen shake, the on-screen controls list (off at first), the **Interface size** (Small, Normal, Large or Larger: how big the menus, the rail, the train's interface, the HUD and the touch buttons are drawn; on a phone Normal is 1.25x the old phone size, Small is the old size, and on a computer Normal is as it always was; on a narrow screen a row that no longer fits wraps onto the next line, a grid loses columns and the page, the rail or the train car scrolls instead of shrinking, see `scripts/reflow.gd`; at Large and Larger the touch buttons keep the size they have at Normal, since a bigger size would leave no room for the game), the **World zoom** (Normal, Closer or Closest: how close the camera is to the climber; a phone also starts a little closer than it used to; the fog, lights and the stair's arrow follow the camera), the **HUD size** (small, normal or large, on top of the Interface size), the **Lighting** quality (Low, Medium or High: see The look below), **To crystals** (the share of banked essence that condenses into crystals at each stair: 0%, 10%, 20%, 35% or 50%; see **Money** under The train), the Entity's **guide tips** (on or off, and **Replay tips**) and **Controller setup** (see Controls above); they're saved. The Entity presents Settings and the Record: its face and a line sit at the top of both. The **Controls** page lists every key and controller button side by side. On a phone the climber screen and inventory tighten up to fit the screen, and scroll where they still can't.

The menus and HUD share one look, taken from the old web game's screens and worked further: a deep violet-indigo night, gilt edges and lettering, warm parchment ink, thin-bordered cards lit faintly from the top, small gothic labels over titles and sections, gilt corner flourishes and double rules with a diamond, and essence colours for essence things. Every title, heading, label, button, tab and name (on cards, over gear on the floor, the beast's bar, the HUD's big words) is set in the gothic **Grenze Gotisch**, always in mixed case since blackletter capitals run together; the text under them in its plain sibling **Grenze**, which stays readable small on a phone (its SemiBold with even-width figures for key badges and numbers that change in place); and words written in the world or quoted (puzzle rule panels, riddles, the Makers' fragments and words, room rules, the Proving's door words, mottos, the title's tagline) in the fine italic **Cormorant Garamond**. They're in `assets/fonts` (SIL Open Font License; also included in the web build), set up once in `ui.gd` as named roles (`ROLES`: display, eyebrow, body, mono, inscription) that every script asks for. The interface colours, fonts, panel, card, button and slot styles (an `Ornate` style box drawn in code: a gradient, a soft shadow, a lit top edge, a gilt hairline and corner flourishes), text sizes and the drawn pieces (bars, essence gems, mark medallions and emblems, key badges, locks, the rail's icons, dividers) live in `scripts/ui.gd`; the item and gear icons are drawn in `scripts/items.gd`, `scripts/gear.gd` and `scripts/vessel.gd` in one shared style from `scripts/icon_art.gd`: slim, colourful shapes with some air round them, smooth gradient shading lit from the top-left, a light violet ink outline scaled to the icon's size, steel with a cool shine and a soft sheen band and gold (the menus' gilt) with a warm one, glass with the liquid inside and a rim light, a faint cool bounce light on the shadowed edges, a soft contact shadow under each icon, and finer detail (grain, seams of stitches on leather, rivets, fullers, twine round a cork) only where the icon is big enough. Named pieces look like themselves: the Coral Trident's three prongs, the Cinder Axe's glowing edge, the Thornmaul's briar, the Wyvern Scaleplate's scales, the beasts' Regarded pieces their own bone, plume and tail. Gear finer than Common glows softly behind its icon in its rarity's colour, brighter the rarer (Fine green, Rare blue, Storied amber, Regarded gold), in its slot and on the floor alike. Each icon is built once per size and copied after that, so drawing them is cheap. Each mark's emblem follows its essence: Air a three-bladed swirl, Water a drop, Fire a flame, Stone a cut gem, Iron a shield, Blade a four-pointed star. The HUD is kept small so the tower gets the screen, in the same look: the bars sit in gilt frames with a dark bezel, a gloss and finials (health turns green while you're poisoned, and health lost to a burned heart or a wound is hatched), the weapon and item slots are chamfered wells, marks are engraved medallions (a red, cracked rim once burned), banners are soft bands between gilt rules, status lines (a shut door, a challenge's rule) sit in cartouches, and a beast's bar is framed with horns and a lingering chunk for the damage just done. The touch buttons are glassy discs in a bezel with a gilt ring (a mark's button fills with essence and glows when it can cast), and every selection wheel (spells, items, a secret bond's tools) is one drawing in `ui.gd` (`draw_wheel`): the world dimmed, a dark disc in an engraved gilt rim, each option in a medallion with its icon, name and cost as gems, the pointed-at one lifted and glowing in its colour. Always there, top left: slim health (with the numbers on the bar) and stamina bars, the weapon in hand and the quick item (small, with its use key and count), and a small medallion per mark that fills with essence and glows once a spell can be cast (on a phone the mark's button lights up too). The rest fades in only when it matters: the essence you've gathered since the last stair (when you pick some up), your three weapons for a moment when you swap, the floor's title when you arrive, the open stair, and for the first seconds of a floor the keys for the menu, climber screen, inventory and controls (H shows every key). The weapon slots, the armor, every item, your attributes and where your next mark is forming are on the climber screen (Tab) and in the inventory (I). On a computer, hovering the mouse over a HUD slot or mark shows what it is and its numbers (a mark or technique: its spell's name, what it does, its essence or fill, how to cast it, and "All your abilities: hold Tab"). **Names in play** (`scripts/ability_hints.gd`): on a computer or with a controller, when a mark or technique fills enough to cast, its spell's name fades in on a small cartouche under its medallion and fades out again; on a phone each cast button carries its spell's whole name (on one line, or two), and a long press on a cast button that opens no ring (a technique, or a mark with one spell) shows a card beside it with the name, what it does and its numbers, and casts nothing (a tap still casts). The first time a climber casts each spell or technique, a one-line toast at the top names it and says what it does ("Ember Bolt: a bolt thrown where you aim; it burns what it hits"), once per spell per climber (kept with the Entity's tips; quiet with tips off). The HUD's always-there part is only redrawn when one of its values changes, and the touch buttons too, which keeps phones fast.

The **dev tools** page is for testing (`scripts/menu.gd`, with the actions in `main.gd`):
- **Edit game data:** the Data editor, at the top (see Editing the game's data below).
- **Marks:** give any bond to any body part with the arrows, and set its step from 1 to 5. Changes are saved, and giving a mark ends the bondless first climb. Fill essence, or clear every mark.
- **Climb:** move to another tower, change floor, or go straight to the tower's beast.
- **Practice a beast:** fight any of the six beasts at once, at full health with full marks.
- **Spawn a monster:** any monster or beast, near you.
- **Gear:** give yourself any weapon or armor (it replaces what's in that slot).
- **Puzzles:** put one kind of puzzle on every floor of rooms (or back to the usual mix), and go to a floor of rooms to try it.
- **Secret essences:** take the Life or Unmaking path at once, bank +25 or +100 flow (to try each step), reset the path, or show the path choice.
- **Secret bonds:** grant the Developer, Designer or Manipulator at any depth from 1 to 5, or clear it; carry +100 essence and +20 flow (to pay for tools); +150 crystal essence (for Tune, Edit, moving marks, a bribe and a change of path).
- **Layout:** **Arrange layout** opens Arrange mode for the HUD and touch buttons, **Arrange menus** for the Dev tools page (F2 with any page open arranges that page; see Arranging the layout below).
- **Cheats:** no dying (a killing blow leaves you at 1 health), heal, clear the room, and show traps (outlines every trap and its reach, hidden ones too).
- **Creator tools: Paint rooms** opens the room painter (see below).

## The room painter (making rooms without code)

**Dev tools → Paint rooms** (`scripts/painter.gd`) paints the climb's hand-made rooms tile by tile, the same small grids as `rooms.gd`, without touching code. It fills the screen:
- **The grid** in the middle: the room, drawn in the game's own tile look (`scripts/painter_tiles.gd`: flagstones, walls with their brick faces, low walls, pillars, chasms, pools, monster spots and the puzzle pieces) in the current tower's colours. Small gilt marks outside its edges show where a corridor can join it. Pinch with two fingers, use the mouse wheel or the Zoom buttons to zoom; two fingers or the middle mouse button move the view.
- **The tools** down the left: Brush, Fill (an area of the same tile), Box (a filled box, dragged corner to corner), Eraser (floor), Mirror left-right and top-bottom (what you paint is copied to the other side), Undo, Redo and Zoom.
- **The palette** down the right: every tile with its look and name (floor, wall, rock, pillar, chasm, water, monster spot, random, and a puzzle room's rail, stone eye, plate, block, gate, lever and reward). Under it **Use in climbs** and **the checks**.
- **The top bar:** Back (to Dev tools), the room's name and state, **Rooms** (every room, yours first, then the built-ins: open, copy, switch on or off, rename or delete yours; built-in rooms can be opened and copied but never changed, and painting one makes your own copy), **Room** (its name, job: start, combat, stair or puzzle, the puzzle and tier for a puzzle room, width and height from 7 x 6 up to 14 x 12, first floor, whether it may flip, which towers may use it, and which tower's look it's shown in), **Save** (Save, Copy rooms and Paste rooms, with a text box), **Try it** and **?** (the Entity's explanation again).

**Controls.** Mouse: click or drag to paint, right-click erases. Touch: tap or drag to paint; tap the buttons. Keys: the arrows move a cursor on the grid, Enter or Space paints (hold it and move to paint along), Delete or Backspace erases, Q and E change the tile, Tab goes to the buttons (and back), Ctrl+Z and Ctrl+Y undo and redo, Esc leaves. Controller: the d-pad or stick moves the cursor, A paints, B erases, LB and RB change the tile, X the tool, Y goes to the buttons (B comes back), Start leaves. On the buttons the usual menu rules apply (`nav.gd`).

**Checks** (`scripts/room_check.gd`) run as you paint and say what's wrong in plain words, outlined on the grid (tap one to find it): a side with no room for a doorway (a corridor needs two open tiles side by side, two deep), a stair with no open floor at the top middle or that can't be reached, a monster spot, a doorway, where the climber starts or a plate that can't be reached, a **low wall that cuts something off** (the game's own check, `rooms.gd` `cover_kept`, which main.gd also uses on every floor: in play such a wall would be left out), and what a puzzle room needs (eyes that can be hit, plates, blocks that can be pushed, a reward behind its gate). Chasms are only real in the Firebird's tower, so a room can pass in one tower and not in another; it's only offered where it passes. A room that fails is a **draft**: it can be saved and tried, but the climb won't use it.

**Try it** drops you into the room at once, in the tower you're in, beside a small hall where you start, with a monster on each monster spot (none in a start room), and you can't fall. The pause key (Esc, Start, Menu) or the stair brings you back to the painter, and the floor you were on is laid out again (`floors.gd` `try_plan`, `main.gd` `try_room`).

**Use in climbs.** A room switched on that passes its checks joins the floor planner's pool for its job (`rooms.gd` `custom`, `for_role`), alongside the built-ins: it can flip, never appears twice on a floor, and only from its first floor and in its towers.

**Keeping rooms** (`scripts/room_store.gd`), the same pattern as the other creator tools:
- Run from the Godot editor, **Save** writes `res://data/rooms_custom.json`. GitHub Desktop then shows the change: write a summary, **Commit to main**, **Push origin**, and the rooms are in every copy of the game, the web build included.
- Anywhere else (the web build, a phone), Save writes `user://rooms_custom.json`, on that device only. **Copy rooms** puts all your rooms on the clipboard and in the text box, to paste to Claude or into **Paste rooms** on the computer (then Save there). Pasting a room with the id of one you have updates it; the rest are added.
- At startup the project's file is read, then the device's on top. Leaving the painter saves what isn't saved yet.

The file is plain JSON, one room per entry, its grid one row per line:

```json
{"format": 1, "rooms": [
	{"id": "painted_1", "name": "Broken colonnade", "role": "combat", "from": 2, "flip": true,
	 "towers": [], "on": true, "grid": [".............", ".o.o.....o.o.", "..."]}
]}
```

`role` is the job, `from` the first floor, `towers` the tower ids that may use it (empty for all), `on` whether it's used in climbs; puzzle rooms add `"puzzle"` (`"eyes"` or `"plates"`) and `"tier"` (1 or 2). The device's file can also list `"deleted"` project rooms. The Entity explains each part the first time (the Archive's "Creator tools").

## Arranging the layout (Arrange mode)

You can move and resize the HUD, the phone's buttons, the menus, the left rail and the train's furniture yourself, by hand, and keep the result. The Entity explains it the first time (and the toolbar's **?** shows the explanation again; it's also in the Archive under **Creator tools**).

- **The HUD and the phone's buttons:** press **F2** during a climb (or **Dev tools → Arrange layout**). The game pauses and everything you can move gets a dashed gold outline with its name.
- **A menu:** open the page you want to change (the inventory, the climber's book, Settings, the pause menu, the Library, the train...) and press **F2** (or **Dev tools → Arrange menus** for the Dev tools page itself). The page stays showing. Point at something and it lights up with its name.
- The toolbar's **Arrange the HUD** / **Arrange the menu** button (top right, when a menu is open) switches between the two.

**Moving things.**
- **Mouse or finger:** drag a thing to move it. Pull one of the small square corner handles of the chosen thing to make it bigger or smaller (it keeps its shape), or turn the **mouse wheel**. It snaps to an 8-pixel grid, to the screen's edges and centre lines, and to the edges of the other things; thin blue lines show what it snapped to. Hold **Shift** to place it freely (on a phone, turn **Grid** off).
- **Things inside things (menus):** a card sits in a column, the column in the page's panel. A click picks the smallest thing under the pointer; **click again in the same spot** for the bigger thing around it (or press **PgUp**; **PgDn** goes back in). Then drag it from anywhere inside it.
- **Resizing (menus):** the page's panel, the rail and the rail's list have long blue **side handles**. Pull the panel's side to make the page wider or narrower (its rows wrap to fit), the rail's right edge to change its width, and the bottom of the rail's list to spread its pages or cars apart. The mouse wheel on the rail changes its text size.
- **Keyboard:** **Tab** picks the next thing (Shift+Tab the one before), the **arrow keys** nudge it (8 pixels; 1 with Shift), **[** and **]** (or the mouse wheel) make it smaller or bigger, **PgUp** / **PgDn** pick the bigger / smaller thing around it, **Ctrl+Z** undoes, **Ctrl+S** saves, **G** turns the grid on or off, **Delete** resets the chosen thing, and **Esc**, **Enter** or **F2** finish.
- **Controller:** the **d-pad** or **LB / RB** pick the next thing, the **left stick** moves it, the **right stick** (up and down) resizes it, **X** undoes, **Y** saves, **View** turns the grid on or off, and **A** or **B** finish.
- **The toolbar** (drag it by its title to move it out of the way; over a menu it starts at the bottom): **Save**, **Copy layout**, **Undo**, **Grid**, **Reset this**, **Reset page** (over a menu: everything showing) and **Reset all** (each reset asks for a second press), **Done**, a line saying what's chosen, where and how big, and at the top right the buttons that switch between the **computer's** and the **phone's** layout and between the menu and the HUD.
- Nothing can be dragged off the screen: HUD things keep at least a corner on it, the phone's buttons stay wholly on it and can't be made too small to tap, a page's panel stays on the screen, what's on a page stays inside the page, the rail's parts inside the rail and a car's furniture inside its car. Leaving with changes you haven't saved asks whether to save them.

**What can be moved.**
- The HUD: health and stamina, the weapon, the quick item, the marks, the path gem and vessel, the unbanked essence counter, the key hints, the floor title, the new floor banner, the beast's health bar, the shut-door and challenge plates, the ring the stair arrow travels along (on a phone the arrow slides along it to stay clear of the touch buttons), and the Entity's bubble.
- The phone: the joystick zone and every button (attack, roll, parry, jump, swap, each mark button, item, next item, take, tools, Bag, Climber, Menu, fullscreen). Where a button is drawn is exactly where it answers a tap.
- Every menu page: its panel (move, scale, make wider or narrower), its title, its tabs or ribbons, each row, grid, column and paragraph, the columns inside a row, and the cards inside them (the inventory's item card, the climber screen's cards, the book's pages, grimoire and technique cards, the appearance page's live preview). This works for every page, new ones too: the game finds them as the page is built. A page with tabs (the climber's book, Settings & Controls) is arranged per tab; the panel is shared by its tabs.
- The left rail: its width, its text size, who you are (the block at the top), the list of pages or cars (and its spacing) and the bottom buttons.
- The train, car by car: each piece of furniture and placard (the heirloom wardrobe, the pack and the chest, the book on its cushion, the outfitter, his shelves, rail, glass case and sign, the bench and its drawers, the tower board, the bookcase and lectern, the signal panel, the brake), the Entity's or the outfitter's words, and the signs and buttons that stand on their own. What's in a piece moves with it. Arrange mode shows the car you're in; to arrange another car, press Done, move along the train and press F2 again.
- Buttons stay buttons: a moved or scaled button is pressed where it's drawn, with the mouse, a finger, the keys or a controller.

**Two layouts.** The computer and the phone each have their own layout (the phone draws everything bigger, at the Interface size, and adds the touch buttons). On a computer, the toolbar's switch shows the phone's layout the way a phone draws it, so you can arrange the phone's buttons from your desk too. HUD things are pinned to the nearest corner or edge of the screen, so they keep their place on other screen sizes, turned sideways, and at every HUD and Interface size (at Large and Larger the phone's buttons and their arrangement shrink together to fit the shorter view, so an arranged button keeps its place). Menus, the rail and the train are kept as a move from their own built-in place, measured as a share of the screen, so a menu arranged on the computer keeps its proportions on a phone; the phone uses the computer's arrangement of them unless you arrange the phone's own.

**Keeping your layout.**
- **Save** keeps it on the device you're using (in the game's own save folder, `user://layout.cfg`). Only that device sees it.
- **To make it everyone's layout** (the one the web version starts with), save it from your computer, inside Godot:
  1. Open the project in Godot and press **F5** to play.
  2. Press **F2** (in a climb for the HUD, or with a menu open for that menu), arrange things, and press **Save**. Because the game is running from Godot, Save also writes the file `data/layout.json` in the project folder (the toolbar says so).
  3. Open **GitHub Desktop**. On the left you'll see `data/layout.json` listed as changed.
  4. At the bottom left, type a short summary, for example `New HUD layout`.
  5. Click the blue **Commit to main** button.
  6. Click **Push origin** at the top. A few minutes later the web version starts with your layout.
- **From a phone (or the web version):** Save can't reach the project, so tap **Copy layout**. It copies the layout to the clipboard and shows it in a box (on a phone: long-press in the box, **Select all**, **Copy**). Paste it to Claude in the chat, and Claude puts it into `data/layout.json` for you.
- **Reset this** / **Reset page** / **Reset all** go back to the shared layout in `data/layout.json` (run from Godot, where Save writes that file, they go back to the built-in layout).

**The file.** `data/layout.json` (and what Copy layout gives) looks like this; an empty set means "as built", and anything not listed stays exactly as built:

```json
		"train.personal.book": {"anchor": "own", "scale": 1.3, "x": -0.05, "y": -0.04}
	},
	"format": 1,
	"touch": {
		"touch.attack": {"anchor": "br", "scale": 1.1, "x": -12.0, "y": -8.0}
	}
}
```

Each entry names a thing and says where it goes:
- `hud.…` and `touch.…` things: the corner or edge it's pinned to (`tl`, `t`, `tr`, `l`, `c`, `r`, `bl`, `b`, `br`), how far its own matching corner sits from the screen's (x and y; in HUD units for `hud.` things, which the HUD size setting multiplies), and its scale. Their list, names and built-in places are in `scripts/layout.gd`.
- `menu.…`, `rail.…` and `train.…` things have the anchor `own`: x and y move them from their built-in place by that share of the screen's width and height, scale grows them about their middle, and `w` / `h` (where they can be resized) are their width / height as a share of the built-in one. Their names are the page (and its tab), then the element: `menu.inventory.row1.column2.card1` is the first card in the second column of the inventory's first row; `train.store.shelf` the Outfitter's shelves. Arrange mode shows each thing's friendly name.

For code: Arrange mode itself is `scripts/arrange.gd`; `scripts/arrange_ui.gd` finds the menus', the rail's and the train's elements as each page is built (`tag_page`, called from `menu.gd` `open`) and puts their arrangement on top of where their containers lay them out. A page needs nothing of its own; a row can name its kind with `set_meta("arrange", "title")` (as `menu.gd` `_header`, `_tabs` and `_footer` do).

## Editing the game's data (no code)

**Dev tools → Edit game data** changes the game's **words, numbers and colours** from inside the game, and keeps the changes for good. No code and no prompts: pick a field, change it, done. The Entity explains the page the first time it opens (and how to keep changes the first time you press Save); the **?** button next to Save explains it again at any time, and its words are kept in the Archive under **Creator tools**.

**Finding things.** The tabs along the top are **Words**, **Numbers**, **Colours**, **Appearance** and **My changes** (Q / E or LB / RB switch tabs). Each tab lists its sections: for Words, the Entity's words, the Makers, items and camps, gear, weapons, bonds and marks, towers and rooms, the train and store, the climb (wings, finds, mirrors, traps), puzzles, the secret essences and bonds, the Unbound, and the title and climbers; for Numbers, store prices, climb pay, mark growth and essence, the climber's core numbers, the marks' spells (every bond's costs, damage, reach and timings, step by step), monsters, items, gear and drops, weapons, the workshop and vessel, wings and rewards, traps, the Makers and the secret essences; for Colours, essences, rarities and items, menus and HUD, the world, tower tints, and monsters and traps; Appearance has the appearance options' names and colours. Or type in the **search box**: it finds any field by its name, its key or what it says ("draught", "price", "stair").

**Changing things.** Each field shows its name, its value and (small, underneath) its key, like `items/ITEMS/draught/heal`.
- **Words**: a text box. Click or tap it (or press its **Edit** button with the keys), type, and press Enter or click elsewhere. On a phone's browser, tapping opens the browser's own text box. Words with blanks such as `%d` or `%s` (where the game puts a number or a name) must keep them, or the change is refused. If you type one of the words kept out of the game ("mana", "spire", "trial", "judgment", "attunement") you get a warning; the change is still kept.
- **Numbers**: a slider (drag it, or Left / Right with the keys or d-pad) and a box to type an exact number in. Numbers stay within sensible limits (usually up to ten times the default), and whole numbers stay whole.
- **Colours**: a swatch and a `#rrggbbaa` box. Press the swatch for sliders: hue, colour, light and opacity.
- **On / off**: a switch.

A changed field turns **gilt**, shows its **default** under it and gets a **Default** button that puts it back. **My changes** lists everything you changed, with Default on each and **Put all back** (it asks for a second press). Most changes show at once (a new item name, a price in the store the next time you look, an Entity tip the next time it's said); a field's row says when it waits for the next page, the next floor or, for a few things read only as the game starts, a restart.

**Keeping changes for good.** Changes save by themselves a second after you make them (and when you leave the page; **Save** does it at once). Where they go depends on where you play:

- **On your computer, running the game from Godot** (open the project in Godot and press the play button or **F5**): Save writes into the project's own file, `data/overrides.json`. To make the changes part of the game for everyone (the web version on your phone too):
  1. Open **GitHub Desktop**. In the **Changes** tab on the left you'll see `data/overrides.json`.
  2. In the **Summary** box at the bottom left, write a few words about what you changed, for example "Cheaper draughts".
  3. Click **Commit to main**.
  4. Click **Push origin** at the top. A few minutes later the web version has your changes.
- **On your phone or in a browser** (the web version): Save keeps the changes **on that device only**, so you can try them. To keep them for good, press **Copy changes**: the changes are copied (on a phone, a box opens: press and hold in it, **Select all**, **Copy**). Paste them into a message to Claude and ask for them to be added to the game. Or, on your computer, run the game from Godot, open Dev tools → Edit game data, press **Paste changes**, paste them into the box, press **Apply**, and then commit and push as above.

**The file.** `data/overrides.json` lists only what's changed, by key, and anything not in it stays as the scripts have it. Colours are written as `#rrggbbaa`:

```json
{
	"format": 1,
	"changes": {
		"entity/TIPS/stair/tip": "A stair. Take it, and what you carry is banked.",
		"items/ITEMS/draught/heal": 45.0,
		"train/SUPPLY_PRICE/draught": 4,
		"ui/GOLD": "#f29a59ff"
	}
}
```

At startup the game reads `data/overrides.json`, then this device's own changes (`user://overrides.json`) on top. A key is the script's file name, the table, then the keys down to the value (`puzzles.lock/TEXT/quiet` for a script in a folder). A value of the wrong kind, a missing key or a number out of bounds is refused or kept in bounds, so a bad file can't break the game. For code: the tables are `static var`s (not `const`) so they can be changed while the game runs; `scripts/data_registry.gd` lists which tables the editor offers (its `SECTIONS`: the friendly name, the tables, which kinds of value) and does the applying and saving; `scripts/data_page.gd` is the page. To offer a new table, turn its `const` into `static var` and add it to a section.

## Climbers and saves

Up to **six climbers** can be kept, each a separate climber with their own everything. **Climbers** on the title screen opens the picker: six slots in two rows of three (on a phone, the same, smaller). Each filled slot is a card with the climber drawn in code in their own look (the climber screen's figure, a soft light on each mark), their name (or "Unnamed climber"), their marks (or **First climb, unmarked**, or **Unbound**), best floor, climbs and when they last played; the climber being played is edged in gold and marked **Playing**. An empty slot shows **New climber**.

- **Choose a card** (click, tap, or the arrows / d-pad and Enter / A; the current climber's card is focused first) to play that climber: on the train after the Regard, or the tower choice for a first climb. **Choose an empty slot** to begin a new climber there: the appearance page, then the bondless first climb.
- **Delete** under a card asks for a second press (it turns red and says so, and a line underneath warns that it can't be undone); the second press forgets that climber for good. With all six full there is no empty slot, and deleting one frees it. Deleting the climber being played makes the most recently played of the others current.
- **Continue** on the title plays the climber played last. **Start over** (title, pause menu, N twice) starts only the current climber over, in their slot; the Record of that slot keeps "the climbers before you". Switching climbers is only from the title (in a climb: Exit to title, then Climbers). The dev tools act on the current climber.

**What is shared and what is each climber's.** Shared by every climber (the settings file, `user://sixfold.cfg`): the settings (music and sound volume, screen shake, Interface size, World zoom, HUD size, Lighting, To crystals, the controls list), controller setup, the Entity's tips on or off, and which climber was played last. Each climber's own (their slot's file, `user://climber_1.cfg` to `climber_6.cfg`): renown (its deeds, titles, medals, bouts, challenges, boons and the special order: the `renown` section; old saves load with none), marks and bonds, essence and soak, density, crystals, the train (stash, pack, Loadout, supplies and what they cost, the store's stock, vessels, the outfitter's last pay), heirlooms, look and name, the Record (and the climbers before them), the Makers' interest and fragments, the secret path and flow, the Unbound, the secret bond, lost pieces, Provings, the kin book, the last tower, and what the Entity has told them (the tips said and the Archive), so a second climber hears the tips again. In the browser these files live in the browser's own storage for the page, as before.

**Saving safely.** Each climber is loaded from their slot's file and remembers it (`profile.gd save_path`), so a climber only ever writes their own file; choosing another climber starts the game again with that climber, and the one before never saves again. The Entity's memory is read again for each climber.

**Saves from before there were slots** move into slot 1 the first time the game starts (`slots.gd migrate()`): a copy of the old file is kept as it was (`user://sixfold_before_slots.cfg`), the climber's sections are copied unchanged into `climber_1.cfg` and read back, and only then does `sixfold.cfg` keep just the shared settings. It happens once; nothing is lost if it can't finish (it tries again next time).

## Appearance: how the climber looks

A **new climber** chooses how they look before the first climb: on the very first launch (after **Begin your first climb** on the title), when an empty slot is chosen on the Climbers page, and straight after **Start over** (from the title, the pause menu or N twice). The page shows a large live preview: the climber as the tower draws it (the same drawing code, scaled up, idling on a patch of floor; **Turn** spins it, or **Q** / **E**, **LB** / **RB**) beside the figure from the climber screen. On the right, an optional **name** (type it, or the **Dice** picks one; on a phone in the browser a tap opens the browser's text box) and five **tabs** of options (the figure leans in on the face for Face and Hair; a dot under a tab means it holds something other than today's look):
  - **Face:** **skin** (7 tones), **eyes** (7 colours), **eye shape** (round, almond, narrow, wide-set), **features** (clear, freckles, a scar, ink lines, war paint, a beauty spot).
  - **Hair:** **hair** (shaved, short, long, tied up, braid, cropped, curls, ponytail, twin braids, crest), **hair colour** (8), **beard** (clean-shaven, stubble, moustache, short, full, braided).
  - **Body:** **build** (slight, average, broad: the chest, shoulders and cloak, in the tower too), **tunic** (8), **trousers** (as the tunic, or 7 colours; in the tower they show as you stride), **boots** (6; worn boots still cover them).
  - **Cloak:** **cloak** (10, today's teal among them; the hood follows it), **cloak style** (long, short, tattered, mantle), **trim** (none or 7 colours: the hem, the mantle's edge and the hood's rim), **pattern** (plain, banded hem, striped, embroidered, patched; in the trim's colour, or a shade of the cloak), and the **hood** up or down.
  - **Extras:** **belt** (leather or black belt, red, blue, green or gold sash, rope), **neck** (nothing, a scarf in the trim's colour, a pendant, a torc), **accessory** (none, earrings, an eyepatch, a feather: in the hood, behind the ear, or a plume on a helm).

The tabs are one row: **Left** / **Right** on it turn them, a click or tap picks one, and Enter or **A** goes into its rows. Up / down run through the name, the tabs, the tab's rows and the buttons in one loop; **Left** / **Right** (the d-pad or stick) change the focused row, and a click or tap on its arrows or a swatch does too; Enter or **A** on a row goes to **Begin**. **Randomise** rolls a whole look (every option; the face, beard, trim, pattern and extras stay plain now and then, `RANDOM_PLAIN`) and keeps the name. Esc does nothing there: **Begin** starts the climb. The Entity explains the page the first time.

Change it later with **Appearance** under the figure on the climber screen's Marks tab, or **Appearance** in your car on the train (beside Climber and Inventory): the same page, ending in **Done** (or Back), which returns where you came from. Every change is kept at once and shows everywhere the climber is drawn: in the tower (every animation and direction: standing, walking, rolling (the cloak's style, trim and pattern on the ball) and fallen (the legs and boots out from under the spread cloak); with the hood down the hair is on show and the hood lies in folds at the neck; a helm or cap still covers the head, and a feather becomes its plume), the mirror image, on the climber screen, the book's body map and the Climbers picker (the name heads it) and in the preview. The face's small details (freckles, a scar) are a pixel or two in the tower at normal zoom; the figure shows them in full. Looks never change how you play.

The options, their names and colours, the tabs, the builds' sizes, the small things' colours (`ACCENTS`), the defaults and the editor's words are data in `scripts/appearance.gd` (`OPTIONS`, `ROWS`, `TABS`, `BUILDS`, `ACCENTS`, `RANDOM_PLAIN`, `DEFAULTS`, `NAMES`, `TEXT`; the names, tabs and colours are in the Data editor's Appearance tab too). The look is saved with the profile (the `look` section) as option ids; a save from before there was a choice, or from before an option existed, loads with today's look for what it lacks (the defaults: trousers as the tunic, brown boots, a long cloak with no trim or pattern, an average build, nothing extra), and unknown ids fall back to it. The drawing reads colours and sizes worked out once per change (`Appearance.look()`), never per frame, in **layers** so worn gear can slot in between: in the tower `draw_legs` (body), `draw_cloak` (clothes), `draw_torso` (body and belt), then the caller's gear (armour, shoulders), `draw_head` (the neck, the head, hair, face and headgear), then the caller's arms and weapon (`player.gd` and the mirror image in `mirror.gd` share them, and `draw_rolled_cloak` / `draw_fallen_body`); on the front figure `draw_front` (the cloak behind, legs, tunic and belt, arms, the mantle, then `draw_front_head`, with `gear_look.gd`'s gear layers between them for a dressed look: see **Worn gear shows on the climber** under Gear), which `menu.gd BodyView._figure` calls for the climber screen, the book, the body map, the picker and the preview. Anything drawn like the climber can call them, or use a `player.gd` node with `set_appearance()` (as the preview's doll does). Tests: `tests/parts/appearance.gd`.

## The Entity: tips, the Archive and the Record

**The Entity** is a small, mysterious presence: a softly glowing orb with two dark eyes and a wry little mouth, drifting sparks and a gentle bob, drawn in code. It watches the climber, as the beast does, but it isn't the beast, and it never explains the story (that's still to be written). Its name and everything it says live in `scripts/entity.gd`, in plain data, so it's easy to rename or rewrite: `TEXT` (its name and the words around it), `LINES` (short lines for moments and pages) and `TIPS` (each tip's line and a fuller account for the Archive, in the groups of `GROUPS`). In the text, `{move}`, `{attack}`, `{roll}` and the like become the keys, controller buttons or touch buttons for whatever the climber is using (`KEYS`).

**Tips.** The first time the climber meets something new, the Entity says a line or two about it in a bubble at the top right (on a phone, under the Bag, Climber and Menu buttons, clear of the joystick and the action buttons; on a short screen it slides left of any cast button). One bubble at a time: the rest wait their turn. A tip never stops the game; it fades after 6 to 12 seconds, and a tap or click on it, **Enter**, or a press of the right stick dismisses it at once. Bubbles wait while a menu is open or the screen is black between floors. Each tip is said once per climber (the "guide" section of the climber's save file: a second climber hears the tips again, with their own Archive; tips on or off is shared). The tips: the Entity itself and the first steps (at the start of a climb), the glint (a monster's first glint), falling, essence (the first essence gathered), banking at the stair, the Regard, casting, your abilities (where the Abilities page is) and the climber screen (a climb with marks); for an Unbound, their techniques (six on the mark buttons, tap to cast, an early cast costs strain) and where to see and change them, overdraw (a burned mark), a new mark, the open stair, rooms and fog, room names, good rooms, monster dens, restless crypts, doors (the first door opened), locked and sealed doors (for doors to come), chasms (near one) and falling through one, camps, the Proving, the beast, the ward, puzzles (each kind, the first time you walk into its room), traps (the first warning), trap rooms, hidden traps, gas vents, poison, gear on the floor, worn pieces (the first helm, ring or other worn piece found), rarities and traits (the first find better than Common), heirlooms, items, the belt (once it's full), the item ring (once you carry two kinds), a fall that loses things, the first lost cache and the inventory.

**Moments.** The Entity also has a short word, in the same bubble, when you fall, when a beast is slain, at a new mark and on reaching the Proving (the first time it's the tip; after that one of a few lines).

**The Archive** (the train's **Library** car, or **Library** on the pause rail in a climb) keeps everything the Entity has told you, grouped by subject (first steps, essence and marks, the tower, puzzles and traps, gear and items, screens), with a count of how many you've heard. Subjects you haven't met show as **???**. Choose one (click, tap, or the arrows and Enter) to read the Entity's line and a fuller, plain account. With tips turned off in Settings the Entity stays quiet, but the Archive still fills in as you meet things. **Replay tips** (in Settings & Controls and the Archive) makes the Entity say everything again; the Archive keeps what it has.

**The Record** (the climber screen's Record tab) is the Entity's record of you: climbs, best floor, marks, floors climbed, monsters slain, beasts slain, falls, puzzles solved, essence banked, heirlooms kept, falls through chasms, the best floor and beasts slain in each tower, and, once you've started over, the climbers before you. The counters are kept with the climber in `profile.gd` (`record`, `note()`, `note_floor()`, `note_beast()`) and start again with a new climber.

**Hooking up a new system.** Call `main.tip("id")` the first time the climber meets it (one line; it's cheap and safe to call every frame) and add an entry for `"id"` to `TIPS` in `entity.gd`. An id with no entry of its own falls back to the part before its last `_` (a new puzzle kind `"puzzle_mirrors"` gets the general `"puzzle"` tip, `"door_iron"` gets `"door"`). Moments use `GuideScript.say("key", {...})` or `GuideScript.moment("tip", "key")`. The bubble, the queue and what's been said live in `scripts/guide.gd`. Tests keep the Entity quiet unless they set the `sixfold_tips` meta on `Engine`. Hovering a HUD slot on a computer shows its tooltip with the Entity's small face in the corner.

## Towers

Every climb starts by choosing a tower (keys 1 to 6, click, or tap a card). Each tower has its own beast, essence, monster mix and look (`scripts/towers.gd`):

| Tower | Essence | Monsters | Look |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| The Gryphon's Tower | Air | archers, bats, harriers and thunderlings, over open drops in the wind; the Gryphon on floor 12 | cold, blue stone and pale torches |
| The Kraken's Tower | Water | slimes, lurkers, shellbacks and deepfins, in water that rises and drains; the Kraken on floor 12 | damp, teal stone and green-white torches |
| The Wyvern's Tower | Iron | knights, cinders, smiths and forge golems; the Wyvern on floor 12 (the hardest) | a forge: workshop halls, molten channels, grated corridors, furnaces (see below) |
| The Manticore's Tower | Stone | scorplings, rams and burrowers; the Manticore on floor 12 | sun-baked sandstone: courts in the sun, shaded alleys, sand (see below) |
| The Firebird's Tower | Fire | wisps and bloomcaps, and chasms to jump; the Firebird on floor 12 | pale, sunlit stone and gold torches |
| The Chimera's Tower | Blade | thornhounds and mongrels; the Chimera on floor 12 | the Thornwild: mossy green stone and pale green torches |

**The Gryphon's tower has a summit.** On floor 12, the summit, the Gryphon itself waits, alone. It rakes with its talons up close (a red wedge, parryable), dives on you from range (it locks onto where you stood when it glints; the landing can't be parried, so roll out of the red circle), and throws a fan of feathers (parryable, and a Crosswind turns them back). It can't be lifted or interrupted, only staggered, and at half health it calls two bats. Beat it, take the stair, and the climb is complete.

**The Kraken's tower has a summit too.** On floor 12 the Kraken waits. It sweeps a tentacle across a wide wedge in front of it (parryable), slams three spots at once with one landing where you stand (can't be parried: roll out of the red circles), and spits a fan of ink (parryable). At half health it calls two lurkers.

**The Wyvern waits atop its tower.** On floor 12 it sweeps its tail across a wedge in front of it (parryable), breathes fire down a narrow cone (can't be parried: roll or step aside) that leaves the floor burning, and sprays a fan of embers (parryable). At half health it calls two cinders.

**The Chimera waits atop its tower.** Three beasts in one: the lion mauls up close (parryable) and breathes fire down a narrow cone (can't be parried: roll or step aside); the goat charges in a straight line (parryable; it's stunned if it charges into a wall); the serpent tail spits three poisoned spikes (parryable). At half health it calls two thornhounds.

**The Chimera's monsters:** the **thornhound** is a fast pack hound that circles, glints and leaps at you with a bite (parryable). The **mongrel** is a patchwork beast that alternates a bite up close (parryable) with a fan of thrown quills (parryable).

**Jumping and chasms.** Everyone can jump (Z): a short hop that costs a little stamina and carries you over chasms and burning floor (it isn't a dodge: attacks still hit you in the air). In the Firebird's tower, floors above the first have **chasms**: walk or get knocked into one and you fall through it: it doesn't hurt, but you land on the floor below and climb again from there (never below floor 1; your unbanked essence stays with you). Walking monsters can't enter chasms; flying ones (bats, cinders, wisps and the beasts that fly) cross them.

**The Firebird waits atop its tower.** On floor 12, in an open hall, it swoops straight through you (parryable), leaving fire in its wake; calls down a firestorm on four spots, one where you stand (can't be parried: get out of the circles, or jump); and throws a fan of embers (parryable). The first time it falls it burns to ashes, can't be hurt for two seconds, and rises again at 40% health. At half health it calls two wisps.

**The Firebird's monsters:** the **wisp** flickers around you, glints and darts straight through where you stood (parryable), leaving a little fire. The **bloomcap** is a rooted fire-fungus: it glints, locks onto where you stand and lobs burning spores that land a moment later (can't be parried: step out of the circle or jump); the ground keeps burning.

**The Manticore waits atop its tower.** On floor 12 it swipes with its claws (a red wedge, parryable), fires a volley of three poisoned tail spikes (parryable), and roars, bursting out around itself (can't be parried: roll through it). At half health it calls two scorplings.

**The Manticore's monsters:** the **scorpling** scuttles up, glints and stings (parryable); a sting that lands **poisons** you: 2 health every half second for a few seconds (the stair's rest cures it). The **ram** lowers its horns, glints and charges in a straight line (parryable). A ram that charges into a wall or pillar is stunned, so letting it miss is an opening.

**The Wyvern's monsters:** the **cinder** is a living ember that flits at range, glints and spits a burning ember (parryable). The **smith** plods up and brings its hammer down in front of it (parryable, but it hits hard), leaving the floor there burning for a few seconds. **Burning floor** costs 4 health every half second you stand in it; rolling through is free.

**The Kraken's monsters:** the **lurker** swims under the water, where only ripples and two eyes show and it can't be hit. It rises near you, glints and lunges (parryable), then stays up for a moment: that's your chance. The **shellback** plods toward you behind a shell that blocks most of a blow from the front (heavy blows crack it), and bashes up close; get behind it.

**The Gryphon's monsters:** the **bat** circles you, then glints and swoops straight through where you stood (parryable). The **thunderling** walks up, stops and sparks, then bursts into a ring of lightning: it can't be parried, so step out of the ring or roll through it.

Every kill also yields 1.5 essence of the tower's own kind, so the tower you choose is how you steer which bonds you'll form. The tower's beast is the one who regards you at the top of your first climb.

### The Wyvern's tower: the forge

Its floors (`scripts/towers/wyvern_floor.gd`, named as the tower's `module` in `scripts/floor_styles.gd`) are a forge: long workshop halls in a grid (more ways between rooms than most towers), carved in its own shapes: **workshops** with benches and anvils in rows and a clear aisle down the middle, **forge halls** with machine columns along both walls, and long plain halls (hand-made rooms turn up about a third as often, so the forge's own shapes win). Every corridor is an **iron grate** over the forge's fires.

- **Molten channels** run right across many halls, and **gutters** of molten metal line the walls of others (never by a doorway). Nothing crosses the metal: walk into it and you stop at its edge, and monsters can't path over it, but you see across it and arrows and thrown embers fly over it (some spells' bolts stop at its edge). Every channel has a **bridge**: a chain bridge of planks laid across. From floor 3, now and then a bridge is **hauled up** (its outline dashed on the metal, its leaf standing on the near bank): strike its **crank** on the side you came in by and it comes down for good. The game proves the stair and the task can always be reached with the channels as rock and a raised bridge as a lever's door (`scripts/reach.gd`), and traps never sit on the metal (trap rooms keep their whole floor for their set pieces: no channels there).
- **Heat.** Furnaces stand in some halls, their bellows breathing: a warning glow, then a hot spell (a ring of heat around the furnace). Linger in the heat and it builds up on you (a ring at your feet fills, "Too hot!"); full, it burns: 3 health every half second until you step out and cool, or the climber's **Burning** state when monsters may give you states (`reactions.gd` `CLIMBER` "enabled").
- **Water barrels** stand by the fires. Strike one and it bursts: the heat around it is **quenched** for 10 seconds (the furnaces go dark), you're cooled at once, foes in the splash are **soaked**, and a forge golem caught in it is quenched. A barrel fills again after a while.

**Its task: Stoke the Furnaces.** Two or three cold furnaces stand in different halls. Strike each one's bellows until it roars (four strikes; it cools back if you dawdle). A waking furnace throws its heat at whoever stands at its mouth, so don't linger.

**Its creature: the forge golem** (from floor 4; `scripts/towers/forge_golem.gd`). A lump of iron with a glowing belly-grate that heats up as it fights: every moment it's after you, every blow it swings and every blow it takes (the seams in its plates glow from dull red to white; a ring at its feet shows how hot). Hot, it hardens (your blows do half), walks faster, its slam (parryable) leaves the floor burning, and now and then it **flares**: a ring of heat bursts out around it (can't be parried: get out of the ring or roll). Full, it must **vent**: it slumps, steaming, for a few seconds and takes double. Water does the same at once: soak a warm golem (a barrel, a pool, a water spell) and it's **quenched**. It carries iron essence.

**Its scenario: Hold the Forge.** Three furnaces burn in the hall; step onto the mark and the doors shut. The fires die down by themselves, and faster with a foe beside them, and waves of the forge's creatures go for them: strike a furnace's bellows to stoke it (or relight it). After 40 seconds: gold if none went out, silver if one did, bronze otherwise; all three out at once ends it.

Every number and word is in the script's `RULES`, `HEAT`, `BARREL`, `FURNACE`, `FORGE` and `TEXT`, and the golem's `GOLEM` (the Data editor's "Towers: the Wyvern's forge and the Manticore's sands").

### The Manticore's tower: sun and sand

Its floors (`scripts/towers/manticore_floor.gd`) are sun-baked sandstone: wide **courts** open to the sky, ringed by a colonnade; **colonnades** (a long nave between two rows of pillars, with side aisles); and **alleys**, mazes of low walls with narrow ways winding between them (again more ways between rooms, and fewer hand-made rooms).

- **Sun.** A shaft of sunlight lies across every court and nave and drifts slowly back and forth across it (about half a minute each way), and the courts lie in a warm open light. Standing in the sun (a small sun shows at your feet) monsters notice you from farther (1.4 times as far).
- **Shade.** The walks under the colonnades, the aisles and the alleys are deep shade (a shade sign at your feet). There monsters notice you only from under half as far, and one that's after you loses track of you (the **Unseen** state, which Unseen Strike and Ambush use) while you stay in the shade more than a few steps away from it.
- **Shifting sand.** Soft **drifts** of sand in the courts slow you a little; a burrower swims in them. **Sand pits** (from floor 3) pull you slowly toward the sink in their middle: reach it and the sand **swallows** you (it hurts) and spits you out at the edge. **Cracked flagstones** crumble into sand after you cross them. None of it ever walls anything off (Sandshaper's Rooted Stance keeps you out of a pit's pull, and fliers aren't slowed).

**Its task: Wake the Sun Seals.** In two or three rooms a polished stone stands under a skylight, its seal set in the floor a few steps off. Strike the stone to turn it (an eighth of a turn a blow). When the drifting patch of sunlight crosses the stone, it throws the light the way it faces: face it at its seal and the seal drinks the light and wakes.

**Its creature: the burrower** (from floor 3; `scripts/towers/burrower.gd`). A sand-coloured, many-legged digger that swims under the sand, seen only as a moving mound and ripples, and can't be struck there. When you stand on sand near it, it marks the spot under you (a ring) and **erupts** out of it (can't be parried: step or roll off the ring), then stays up a moment: your chance. It can only swim in sand: stand on stone and it must come up to reach you, **exposed** ("Exposed!"): slow, taking half again from every blow, only able to bite (parryable). So lure it onto the stone. It carries stone essence.

**Its scenario: the Sinking Court.** Step onto the mark and the court's floor starts giving way to quicksand behind you, rolling toward the far gate. Only the old stones hold: a winding path of them, and a few more. Quicksand slows you and drags you under: stand in it too long and it swallows you (a hit, and you're thrown onto the nearest stone). Reach the far gate: gold for no hits and a quick crossing, silver for one hit, bronze otherwise; leaving the court ends it.

Every number and word is in the script's `SUN`, `SHADE`, `SAND`, `SEAL`, `SINK` and `TEXT`, and the burrower's `BURROWER`.

### The Kraken's tower: flooded floors

The Kraken's floors are built by their own script, `scripts/towers/kraken_floor.gd` (named as the tower's `module` in `scripts/floor_styles.gd`). Its numbers, words and colours are in the Data editor ("The Kraken's water and the Gryphon's heights": `WATER`, `TEXT`, `LOOK`).

- **Layout.** Rooms are mostly round and oval (`own_shapes` in `floor_styles.gd`; hand-made rooms are rarer here), a little smaller than elsewhere so the corridors between them are longer, and the corridors **wind**: each long enough corridor wanders up to three tiles sideways and back (two tiles wide, three at a bend), drawn as a curving channel of water. Round rooms often have a **deep pool** in the middle with a walk all round it.
- **The water.** Every room and corridor is a basin with a **water level** drawn in code: the water spreads across a room as it rises, ripples move on it, a **level line** shows on the room's north wall with a pale **tide mark** above it where it last stood, and salt crusts the floor where it reached.
  - **Tides:** most rooms and corridors fill and drain on a cycle, never deeper than **shallow**; the cycles lag from room to room, so the tide seems to roll through the floor. Shallow water slows you (unless you wear Wading Boots or have a water bond) and soaks foes that wade in it (soaked foes conduct lightning: see States and reactions).
  - **Deep water** can't be crossed on foot: you and walking monsters stop at its edge, and monsters path round it. **Pools** stay deep. A **loop corridor** (a second way round) may flood deep at high tide; if it rises while you stand in it, the water sweeps you out.
  - **Sunken rooms:** now and then a dead-end room lies under deep water with its chest on the bottom. A **sluice wheel** in the room next to it drains it: stand at the wheel until it turns. The water rushes into the wheel's own room for a while, and the sunken room can be walked (its guards are swimmers: when it drains they're stranded).
  - Deep water is never the only way to the stair (the planner checks every floor: a pool or a flooding corridor may only ever take a second way), except for the task below.
- **The task: Drain the Stairwell** (`sluices` in `objectives.gd`, only in this tower). The passage into the stair room is flooded deep. Turn every sluice wheel on the floor (stand at each until it turns; a blow sets it back) and each one lowers the water a step; with the last, the stairwell drains and the ward lifts.
- **The deepfin** (`scripts/towers/deepfin.gd`, from floor 3). It lives in deep water: only a fin and its wake show as it shadows you along the bank, and nothing can touch it down there. Come near the water and it rises, glints and **leaps out** at you, jaws first (parryable). Then it's stranded on the stone, thrashing: soaked through, so lightning arcs through it, and every blow lands half again as hard, until it drags itself back. Too long out of the water (its pool drained) and it dries out and weakens. With no deep water on a floor it keeps to the shallows as a lurker does. Drops water essence.
- **The scenario: the Filling Room** (`flood` in `scenarios.gd`, only in this tower; likelier than the shared scenarios here). Step onto the mark: the doors shut and the water comes in while lurkers and the tower's monsters come for you. Reach the drain wheel at the far end and turn it (a blow knocks your hands off it for a moment) before the water fills the room. Graded on the hits you take and how high the water got.

### The Gryphon's tower: open heights

The Gryphon's floors are built by `scripts/towers/gryphon_floor.gd` (the Data editor's "The Kraken's water and the Gryphon's heights": `VOIDS`, `GUST`, `TEXT`, `LOOK`).

- **Layout.** Big open halls with **ledges and balconies over open drops**: a hall may have a round **well** in the middle with a balcony all round it (sometimes a bridge straight across), a **ledge** where one side falls away, or a **span** of drop right across it with one narrow bridge. Long corridors become **bridges**: the rock beside them falls away, so you cross a plank walkway over the drop. Beyond the halls' walls there's no rock to see, only open air: every hall stands like a balcony.
- **Seeing down.** Through every drop and past every wall you see the floors below, drawn in code: two layers of halls, smaller and dimmer the deeper they are, with their flagstones, walls and torches, sliding with parallax as you move, with tiny shapes walking them.
- **Falling.** Step or get knocked off an edge and you fall to the floor below ("YOU FELL", the same as the Firebird's chasms). Walking monsters keep out of the drops; fliers cross them. Drops never cut anything off: they only ever take tiles away (the planner checks every floor).
- **Wind.** Every few seconds a gust blows across the open spans: streaks of wind show which way a moment before, then it shoves whoever stands near a drop (you and foes) that way. Brace by walking against it. A foe thrown into the air (lifted, or knocked up) is carried over the edge and falls when it comes down; a flier stunned over a drop (a parried bat or harrier) falls too.
- **The task: Turn the Wind Vanes** (`vanes` in `objectives.gd`, only in this tower). No bridge reaches the stair room: its passage is an open gap. Strike each wind vane round (two blows each); when the last turns, a steady wind blows across the gap and you walk over on it.
- **The harrier** (`scripts/towers/harrier.gd`, from floor 3). A raptor that soars high over the halls (its shadow sweeps the floor), folds its wings and dives at you with a glint. Its strike throws you the way it flew: toward the edge, if you stand on one. After the dive it stands on the floor a moment (your opening) before it climbs back up. **Parry the dive on a ledge** and it tumbles over the edge and falls. Drops air essence.
- **The scenario: the Long Bridge** (`bridge` in `scenarios.gd`, only in this tower, in dead-end halls). The hall falls away but for a landing by the door, one long bridge and a perch at the far end. Step onto the mark and cross while gusts blow across the bridge toward the drop and harriers dive out of the open air. Graded on the hits you take and your time; leave the hall and it's over.

Tests: `tests/parts/towers_kraken_gryphon.gd` (layouts, reachability over dozens of floors, the water and the wind, both tasks, both creatures, both scenarios, and the floor bot on both towers). The floor bot sweep can be narrowed to one tower: `godot --headless --fixed-fps 60 -s tests/floor_bot_sweep.gd -- 2 tower=kraken`.

### Giving a tower its own floors: the content contract

Every tower with floors of its own (today the Gryphon, the Kraken, the Wyvern and the Manticore) works the same way, so a new one plugs in without touching shared code:

1. **Its floor script** is `scripts/towers/<tower>_floor.gd` and `extends "res://scripts/towers/tower_floor.gd"`. That base file lists every hook and does nothing in each one, so a tower overrides only what it needs. Don't declare `var main` (the base has it).
2. **Name it** in the tower's entry in `scripts/floor_styles.gd` as `"module": "res://scripts/towers/<tower>_floor.gd"`. Room shapes of its own go in `SHAPE_MODULES` (shape name → script) and are carved by its `carve(shape, size)`. Other style keys it can set: `shapes`, `own_shapes`, `gallery`, `room_grow`, `prefab`, `loops`, `corridor`.
3. **Planning hooks** (static, called while a floor is planned):
   - `shape_corridors(infos, links, corridors, walk) -> Array` returns the corridors' walkable rects. One straight rect per link comes in. The hook may record `link["tiles"]` and `link["path"]`.
   - `shape_plan(plan, infos, links, floor_num, tower)` lays the tower's ground over the finished plan. Set `plan["ok"] = false` to have the floor planned again. It writes:
     - `plan["tower_floor"] = {"module": <its path>, ...}`. `main.gd` makes the node from this.
     - `plan["impassable"]`: Rect2s nobody walks. `reach.gd` and the traps treat them as rock. Today: deep pools, flooding corridors, open drops, molten metal.
     - `plan["gates"]`: `[{tiles, lock_id, opens: {any: [tiles]} or {all: [tiles]}}]`, ground that's shut until what opens it is reached. `reach.gd` counts each one as a locked door, and the traps keep off it. Today: the Kraken's moat and sunken rooms, the Gryphon's gap, the Wyvern's raised bridges.
   - Shared checks that help: `scripts/towers/water_wind_grid.gd` has `cuts()`, which checks that impassable ground cuts nothing off, with doors open and with locked doors shut.
   - `place_task(kind, spec, infos, cands, dist, monsters, floor_num, cell)` places the pieces of its own task kind. The default spreads them over `cands` the plain way (`objectives.gd place_spread`).
   - `scenario_plan(kind, info, gates, floor_num, cell)` returns its own scenario's marks, or `{}` when the room won't do.
4. **Its own task kind** goes in `objectives.gd KINDS` with `"towers": [<tower>]` and `"module": <its path>`.
5. **Its own scenario** goes in `scenarios.gd SCENARIOS` with `"towers"`, `"module"`, optional `"seal"` (the doors shut while it runs) and `"weight"`. Give it an `scn_<kind>` room in `themes.gd`.
6. **Hooks in play** are called on `main.tower_floor`, the node made fresh each floor:
   - building: `setup(plan)`, `clear()`
   - ground: `blocks_walking(tile)` (also counts in `main.is_solid`), `slow_at(pos)`, `wet(pos)` (shallow water: `main.in_water`), `sight_k()` (how far monsters notice you), `is_void(pos)` (an open drop), `reach_live(solid, size, doors, opens)` (for the safety net)
   - the dev tool: `open_gates()` (Open all doors opens its gates too)
   - swings: `strike(from, dir, reach, arc)` (every melee swing)
   - its task: `task_tick(ob, delta, p, hurt)`, `task_strike(ob, from, dir, reach, arc)`; it draws its own pieces
   - its scenario: `scenario_begin(s, host)`, `scenario_tick(s, delta, p, host)` (calls `host._finish(s, grade)`), `scenario_title(s)`, `scenario_status(s)`, `scenario_goal(s)`; it draws it too
7. **Floor bot hooks** (`tests/floor_bot_driver.gd`):
   - `bot_step(bot)`: a step before anything else (a crank)
   - `bot_task(bot)`: its own task's next step
   - `bot_grid(astar, bot)`: costs and walls on the bot's grid
   - `bot_avoid()`: tiles never to step on
   - `bot_steer(pos, d)`: adjusts the stick (bracing against wind)
8. **Its creature** is a script that `extends "res://scripts/enemy.gd"`, listed in `enemy.gd SUBKINDS` and spawned with `EnemyScript.make(kind)`. It overrides what it needs: `setup`, `_act`, `_draw_own`, `_overlay_extra`, `take_hit`, `on_parried`. Give it an entry in `enemy.gd TYPES`, `floors.gd MONSTERS`, `essence.gd DROPS`, `gear.gd FAVOUR`, the tower's `weights`, and sounds in `sound_bank.gd` (`hurt_<kind>`, `kill_<kind>`).
9. **Data editor:** put its tables in `static var`s and register them in `data_registry.gd`.
10. **Drawing and sight:**
    - `paint_layout(room_view, batch) -> bool`: draws the floor's walls, rock, cover and door posts itself (`room.gd`).
    - `sight_open() -> PackedByteArray`: rock the eye sees across, one byte per grid tile, read as each floor starts (`fog.gd`).
    - Anything else it draws, it draws on its own node (z under the climber), or on a child it adds to `main.room_view` (as the Kraken's water does, under the pillars).

**Older per-tower hooks map onto these:**

| Older hook | Use instead |
| --- | --- |
| a style's "builder", `after_build(plan, infos, links, floor_num, tower, style)` | the style's `module` and `shape_plan(plan, infos, links, floor_num, tower)` (the style is `floor_styles.gd for_tower(tid)`) |
| a style's "script", `finish_plan`, `script_for` | the same |
| an organic `carve()` | `carve()` with the shape in `SHAPE_MODULES` |
| the "prefab" factor | the style's `prefab` |
| `main.tower_systems` / `tower_call(name)` | the one `main.tower_floor` and its hooks (a tower with several systems runs them inside its own node) |
| `tower_call("strike")` from `player._melee` | `strike()` |
| a `water_slow` factor | `slow_at()` (and `wet()` for shallow water) |
| `main.sight_open` / `fog.see_open` | `sight_open()` |
| how far you're seen | `sight_k()` |
| `room.gd layout_painter.paint()` | `paint_layout()` |
| "towers"/"script" task kinds with custom delegation | "towers"/"module" kinds, `place_task` / `task_tick` / `task_strike` (the kind draws itself) |
| "towers"/"script" scenarios and `handler()` | "towers"/"module" scenarios and the `scenario_*` hooks |
| creature hooks | an `enemy.gd SUBKINDS` script |
| `bot_go` | `bot_steer()` (or `bot_step()` for a step of its own) |
| a custom `bot_step` | `bot_step()` |
| `bot_grid` | `bot_grid()` |
| `no_foot` / `fluid_locks` / `lever_gates` | `impassable` and `gates` |

## Marks, essence and the Regard

- **Six body parts**, one for each of the six towers: hand, legs, heart, brow, throat and back. Each holds one mark. A **backstab** (striking a foe that faces away from you, because it has committed to an attack or you rolled behind it) deals 50% more damage.

- **Your first climb is bondless.** You start clumsy, with weapons only. Climb 3 floors.
- **Essence:** monsters leave essence when they fall: Air, Water, Iron, Fire or Stone (slimes Water, archers Air, knights Iron, cinders and the Wyvern Fire). It flies into you and is **banked at the next stair** (a share condenses into crystals there: see **Money**). Fall before the stair and the unbanked essence is lost.
- **Your body soaks it up** through what each part does: the **hand** from weapon strikes (+0.5 per hit), the **legs** from rolling through attacks (+4), the **heart** from taking hits and surviving (+4), the **brow** from parries (+6, perfect +9), the **throat** when one swing or spell hits several foes (+2 for each foe past the first), the **back** from backstabs (+3) and finishing blows (+2). Unmarked parts glow faintly as they soak, and the climber screen's Marks tab (Tab) shows each unmarked part's soak, the leader ("your next mark is forming"), and which bond it would be right now.
- **Where and which:** your play style decides the body part (the one you used most), and the essence you gathered decides the bond. No bond has a home body part. When two bonds share an essence (say, the three Air bonds), the way you fought this climb breaks the tie: lots of parrying leans Hunter, lots of striking Stormcaller, lots of dodging Windwalker.
- **The Regard:** at the top of your first climb the tower's beast takes notice. Your most-used body part takes its first **mark**, and the essence you gathered most decides which bond it is. If you fall on the first climb you're still regarded, but the mark is **faint**: weaker (75% power, 25% dearer) until it has soaked up 80 more essence. Dismissing the Regard first asks whether you accept the mark: refusing it makes you **Unbound** (see **The Unbound**). Break a barrier on the first climb and there's no question: the beast **refuses** to mark you and you are Unbound.
- **Growth across climbs:** banked essence slowly strengthens every mark you carry, up to step 5: a mark's own kind counts most, the rest only a little, and the more marks your body carries the slower each grows (`STEP_XP`, `AFFINITY_WEIGHT`, `OTHER_WEIGHT`, `STEP_RATE`, `BODY_DRAG` in `profile.gd`). **Winning the Proving** (floor 6) raises one mark a step: the one whose body part you used most that climb. Your marks and their growth are saved.
- **Later marks come only at a summit, at most one a climb.** Unmarked body parts fill like batteries: each soaks up only part of what it does (`SOAK_RATE`), the fuller it is the slower it fills, and a body that already carries more marks fills every part slower still. When you reach a summit and the tower's beast takes notice, a full part (`MARK_FULL` of `NEW_MARK_SOAK`) takes a new mark, shaped by the essence you've banked since your last one. The aim: twenty climbs or more for all six marks (the numbers are first guesses).
- **Each bond is a kit:** a **Body** (an upgrade to a basic action), a **Passive**, and **spells learned as it grows** (new spells at steps 1, 2, 4 and 5; step 3 changes the first). **Tap** a mark's key to cast its selected spell; **hold** it to open a ring of its spells (time stops), point at one and let go to cast it. ## States, reactions and named pacts

Marks work together. Spells leave **states** on a foe, two states that meet set off a **reaction**, and two marks that answer each other form a **named pact**. Every name, line, colour and number is in data, in the Data editor's **States, reactions and pacts** sections (words, numbers and colours): `scripts/reactions.gd` (`STATES`, `REACTIONS`, `RULES`, `CLIMBER`) and `scripts/pacts.gd` (`PACTS`, `GRAFT_POWER`, `TEXT`).

**States.** A foe can be **charged**, **airborne**, **soaked**, **burning**, **poisoned**, **bleeding**, **slowed** or **unseen** (it has lost track of you: smoke, a decoy, Vanish, Ashfall, Steam, Mist). They live in one place, the foe's `states` dictionary (`enemy.gd`): state to seconds left. The foe's older fields are that same dictionary under their old names (`charged`, `airborne`, `burn_t`, `venom_t`, `chill_t`, `lost_t`), so everything that already charged, lifted, burned, poisoned, slowed or blinded a foe gives it the state too: Stormcaller's lightning and Static arcs, Gale Lift and the cyclones, venom, of Embers and burning ground, a Coral Trident's chill, Ashwalker's smoke and decoys. New: **soaked** (Tidecaller's waves, Riptide, Carry, the Maelstrom, Flood, Geysers, Tidebreak's waves, Deepcaller's Undertow, Anchor, Leech and Drown, the Unbound's Water and its Chill, Current and Mist, and any foe standing in a pool) and a general **bleeding** (`enemy.gd bleed(dps, seconds)`: Surgeon's Sever and Bloodletting, the Unbound's Blade, Rend and Answer, Dust Devil's shards). Fire burns (Fireball, Cluster, Meteor, Firewall and every burning floor, Fire Lash, Overheat, Wingburst, the Heat Aura, an ignited Smoke Veil, a kindled weapon). A heavy blow on a foe in the air is a **slam** (Quake, Downdraft, a finisher, an explosion), and a Deepcaller's drains and a Lifeline are a **drain**: moments, not states. Each state shows as a small badge over the foe's head, a glyph of its own in its colour (a bolt, an arrow up, a drop, a flame, bubbles, a red drop, a snowflake; it blinks as it runs out), with drips, flames or blood on the body; unseen keeps its "?".

**Reactions.** When a state lands on a foe that holds the other of a pair, the reaction fires once (then waits its `again` seconds on that foe) and its word rises over the foe:

| States that meet | Reaction | What happens |
| --- | --- | --- |
| soaked + charged | Conduct | lightning jumps to every soaked foe within 140; each is struck, stunned 1 s and dries off |
| soaked + burning | Steam | a cloud where it stood; foes inside lose track of you (both states go) |
| burning + poisoned | Fumes | the poison spreads to the foes beside it as burning gas (half its stacks, and burning) |
| airborne + slam | Crater | slammed out of the air: a big hit, and the foes around it are struck and knocked down |
| airborne + charged | Skybolt | a bolt from above strikes it, and it falls |
| bleeding + drained | Hemorrhage | the drain is doubled |
| slowed + burning | Smoulder | the burn lasts twice as long |
| unseen + poisoned | Ambush | your first poison on a foe that has lost track of you: 5 more stacks (smoke on a foe already poisoned is no ambush) |

Reaction damage grows 15% for each step of your strongest mark past the first (`RULES per_step`); a chain of reactions setting each other off through a crowd stops after 4 (`RULES depth`). Reactions fire on monsters only. Whether monsters can give *you* states is still the owner's question: it is built but **off** (`CLIMBER "enabled"` false: nothing lands on the climber). Switched on in the Data editor, each monster kind in `CLIMBER "from"` would leave its state on a blow, and a reaction on you would hurt.

**Named pacts.** A pact holds while you carry both of its marks, wherever they sit; the first time it forms it is announced (its name over you, and the Entity's tip) and remembered (`profile.gd pacts_formed`, an old save has none). Your book's **Marks** section lists the pacts you hold; the **Library** has a shelf of those you've formed (the rest as ???).

| Pact | Marks | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Tempest | Stormcaller + Tidecaller | your waves leave foes charged as well as soaked (so they Conduct) |
| Dust Devil | Windwalker + Sandshaper | Quake throws foes up; Gale Lift leaves shards that cut and bleed what walks over them |
| Whirling Steel | Blade Forger + Windwalker | conjured blades lift light foes |
| Forge Runes | Flameblade + Rune Artist | kindled strikes leave small fire runes (they arm after a moment and burn what sets them off) |
| Wildfire | Flame Contractor + Flamewing | burning ground anywhere feeds the aura's heat |
| Undying | Warden + Rekindler | Last Ember also raises a barrier |
| Bloodletter | Deepcaller + Surgeon | draining a bleeding foe heals double |
| Ambusher | Hunter + Ashwalker | unseen shots always strike a weak point |
| Toxic Precision | Venomist + Surgeon | Sever adds 3 poison stacks |
| Menagerie | Covenant + Grafter | your creatures carry your newest graft's power (hand: harder blows, legs: faster, heart: they mend, brow: blows stagger, throat: blows splash, back: blows poison) |

## Secret essences: Life and Unmaking

Two essences aren't like the six (`scripts/secret.gd`, where every word and number lives). **Ordinary monsters never leave them: only great foes do.** A tower's beast leaves mostly Life and some Unmaking; the Proving's shadow of the beast the same split, but less; a champion (a sealed wing's, or the Proving's sentinel) a little of one or the other, by tower (Life from the Kraken's, Manticore's and Firebird's towers, Unmaking from the Gryphon's, Wyvern's and Chimera's). A mirror image of the climber (see **Mirror images**) leaves mostly Unmaking and some Life. Amounts grow with the floor like other essence. None drop on the bondless first climb. They rise from the foe as big glowing motes in their colour (Life green, Unmaking violet) and fly to you. They aren't currency: the crystals, the store, the bench and density never list them.

**Choosing a path.** The first time a great foe falls and its secret essence rises (usually the Proving's shadow on floor 6), the game stops on a choice: let in **Life** ("you endure") or **Unmaking** ("you take apart"). The Entity says what's at stake, and each card shows a climber standing in that essence's shadow and what each step does. The choice is for good, so a card is taken with a **second** press or tap (the first only picks it, and presses in the first moment are ignored, so a tap meant for the fight can't choose). It's saved with the climber; later, only the Developer mark or pleading with a Maker will be able to change it (`main.gd reset_path()` is the hook; the dev tools use it now).

**Only your path's essence soaks in.** The other still rises from great foes and visibly streaks through you and out the far side, unbanked, unless you carry a **vessel** (below).

**The vessel** (`scripts/vessel.gd`, where its rules, numbers and words live; its name, "Vessel", is a placeholder in `items.gd`; `scripts/vessel_page.gd` puts it on the train). A sealed glass vessel bound in brass, drawn in code: a round glass belly in brass bands with a sealed domed cap, filling with what it holds (Life green, Unmaking violet; a full one glows and glints). It's rare: a **Rune Artist brews one at the bench** (the Vessel counter: 60 Iron, 45 Water, 45 Air, `BREW`), and very rarely (**4%**, `VAULT_CHANCE`) a vault's full prize offers one as its third card (never on the first climb, nor while you carry one). It sits on the belt like other items (one kind of item, never stacked, never swapped out by the belt's take card), hangs at the climber's hip glowing with what it holds, and while you carry one a small vessel and gauge sit beside the health bar (hover for what it holds). Using it only says what it holds.
- **Capturing.** Once you have a path, the other secret essence that rises from a great foe streaks at you as before, but curls round the vessel at your hip and sinks into it ("+12 Unmaking into the vessel (12 / 30)"). It holds **30** (`CAPACITY`), of one kind (the first it took); whatever doesn't fit passes through you as before. Your path's essence soaks into you as always, and before a path is chosen it catches nothing.
- **Unbanked, until home.** Fall and the vessel and all it holds are lost with the belt (the fall screen says so). Survive the climb or read a recall scroll and it comes home, filled or not, into your **storage chest** (after the chest's pieces; it's never broken into crystals with the rest of the belt). A vessel's card in the chest packs it for your next climb (**Take it on your next climb**: it goes on your belt as you depart, takes a belt kind, and keeps filling) or leaves it.
- **A full vessel**, three uses, each the player's choice (the owner hasn't picked one yet):
  - **Trade it** at the store: **Sell to the outfitter** lists your vessels, and a full one trades for **120 crystal essence of one kind you choose**, or **30 of every kind** (180 in all) (`TRADE`). Each button asks once.
  - **Work it into a piece** at the bench (a Rune Artist, on the **Vessel** counter): **temper a +3 piece to +4**, one rank past the bench's limit (each rank still 25% of its traits' base and 4% for a weapon; `gear.gd RANK_CAP`), or cut a **deep rune**, a lasting rune at the Inscribe spell's full strength, twice a bench rune: Sharpness +20% strikes, Warding +16% protection, Swiftness +10% walk (`gear.gd LASTING_RUNES "deep_*"`; it replaces any rune there, and gear's caps still apply). Either uses up a full vessel from your chest.
  - **Offer it.** Carried up full on your belt, a Maker's meeting adds a card, **Offer the vessel**: a plea for a change of path that's likelier granted (+35%, at most 95%; `makers.gd PLEA_ODDS`). The Maker keeps the vessel whether it agrees or not (`vessel_back` true would hand it back on a refusal). And on the train the Developer's **bribe** can offer a full vessel from your chest, likelier taken than crystals (+25%, at most 90%; `secret_bonds.gd BRIBE`); like any bribe, nothing is taken if it refuses.
- The Entity explains the first vessel, the first capture and the first full vessel. Vessels in the chest are saved with the train (`train`/`vessels`; old saves load with none). The dev tools (Secret essences) give an empty or a full vessel.

**The flow.** Your path's essence never settles in a body part, never takes a mark and never pushes out a bond. It's one measure, the flow: carried unbanked during the climb (half of it stays when the Proving casts you down; a fall or a recall scroll loses it) and banked at the stair. Banked flow reaches **steps at 25, 75, 150 and 300**, each a real change in play:

| Step | Life | Unmaking |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Health regrows: 0.5 a second (0.75, 1, 1.25 at later steps) | 12% more damage (strikes and spells), 8% less max health (20/13%, 28/18%, 36/22% at later steps) |
| 2 | Every kill mends you: 4 health (5, 6 later) | An aura: awake monsters beside you take 3 damage a second (4, 5 later) |
| 3 | Healing is 50% stronger (60% at step 4): draughts, springs, your marks' heals, weapons that heal on a kill | Your strikes ignore armour: a shellback's shell doesn't turn them, a knight's guard doesn't shrug off light blows (beasts still don't flinch) |
| 4 | Once a floor, a killing blow leaves you at a quarter of your health instead, in a green burst that throws back the monsters near you ("Life will not let you fall") | A slain foe comes apart: 30% of its max health (at most 60) to the monsters beside it (an undone foe doesn't undo again) |

**It lives in your shadow.** With a path, the shadow under the climber holds it, drawn in code and growing darker, larger and more alive (or more broken) at every step. Life: the shadow turns living green and pulses with a heartbeat, sending a bright ring out with each beat; from step 2 roots reach past its edge, from step 3 small leaves sway at their tips and trail behind you as you move, and grass-light flickers where it falls (a soft green light at your feet). Unmaking: the shadow goes violet with a splintered edge, cracks like fractured glass, its edges flaking into rising motes; from step 3 it drinks the light (the darkness layer gathers in a ring around you). Before the first step it's only a faint tint.

**On screen.** Your path's gem appears beside the health bar (with four pips for its steps; hover it for what they do) only once banked flow reaches the first step, and then your unbanked flow leads the essence counter. Before that, the HUD shows nothing and the climber screen only a faint sign. The climber screen's **Essence** tab shows the flow (the shadow, the step, what each step does, how much to the next and what you carry unbanked), and on the **Marks** tab the figure stands in its shadow. The Entity explains the secret essences, the choice, the other essence passing through, and each step the first time.

## The Unbound

Climbers who refuse the tower beasts' marks (`scripts/unbound.gd` holds every word and number about them and their profile state; `scripts/technique.gd` the techniques; `scripts/unbound_run.gd` runs them in a climb; `scripts/technique_page.gd` is their pages). Old saves load as marked climbers.

**Becoming Unbound.** It happens only on the bondless **first climb**, and only once. **Don't break the rules and you are offered:** at the Regard, dismissing it asks first, **accept the mark** or **refuse it**. Refusing takes a second press (and none in the first moment), since there's no road back; the mark the beast just gave is taken back. **Break the rules and you are refused:** with your **fists out** (key 1), hold a strike against a barrier: a **warded stair**, a **vault's seal** or a **lock-in room's shut doorway**. The very first time ever, it only shudders with a faint crack (the warning; the Entity says what breaking one means). After that each held blow (0.45 s) drains 4 unbanked essence, hurts your hands for 5 (10 with almost nothing to drain; never the last of your health) and cracks it: 4 blows break a ward or a seal, 3 a doorway. Breaking through skips the challenge and forfeits its reward (the stair opens with no puzzle counted and no puzzle bonus; a vault stands open and empty, its room's question ended). **One barrier broken on the first climb breaks the tower's rules:** at the Regard (reached or fallen) the beast **refuses** to mark you: a "REFUSED" screen, no mark and no choice, and you are Unbound. **After the first climb** a marked climber can't become Unbound: their fists only make a barrier shudder ("It shudders, and holds"), and summits never offer a refusal. The Unbound still break barriers later (the Proving's sealed reliquary too) with raw essence (below). Old saves' barrier counts are dropped when they load. Being Unbound is for good, except through the **Developer**: its holder gets a "Mark me again" button on the Core tab (on the train, two presses), which puts the densest part's raw essence into a bond there (chosen by what's pooled in it) and keeps half of the rest as soak, so later marks come sooner. Raw essence against barriers (the Unbound): from a step away with any melee weapon or fists, 6 unbanked essence a blow, no hurt, two cracks a blow.

**Raw essence in the body.** Banked essence pools raw in the six parts (the profile's `part_density`): Blade the hand, Air the legs, Stone the back, Water the throat, Fire the brow, Iron the heart. At the train you can **route** a kind to another part (from then on). Your path's essence (Life or Unmaking, the banked flow) runs through all six. A part's raw density sets its **level**, 1 to 10 (at 0, 25, 60, 110, 170, 250, 350, 470, 610, 780): its techniques hit up to **twice as hard** at 10, it combines **one essence, two from level 4, three from level 8**, and it gives a **gift at level 5** (hand: strikes 15% harder; legs: walk 8% faster; heart: 15% more health; brow: techniques cost 25% less; throat: stamina back 20% faster; back: strain fades twice as fast). The total sets the **stage**: Stir, Pulse, Flow, Surge, Torrent, Tide, Tempest, Maelstrom, Cataclysm, Unbound (at 0, 60, 150, 300, 500, 800, 1200, 1700, 2400, 3300), shown on the climber screen. They never take tower bonds; secret bonds still come from the Makers. At a **summit** they take no mark: a card screen lets them **condense** 60 of the tower's essence into a part of their choice, then the victory.

**Techniques.** The Unbound build their own spells: a **body part and one to three essences, the first leading.** The part gives the shape: the hand a **strike** or a thrown **bolt**, the legs a **dash** (untouchable, striking what you pass) or a **leap**, the back a **shell** or a **push**, the throat a **shout** in a cone or a **pull**, the brow a **mark** (the foe takes 50% more damage) or **sight** (every foe shows through walls and fog), the heart something on yourself. Each essence adds what it does: Fire burns, Water slows (or heals you over time), Air reaches farther and throws harder (and carries a dash farther), Stone staggers and guards, Iron pierces armour and drags foes to you, Blade cuts deeper and bleeds, Life heals you, Unmaking ignores armour and erodes at a small cost in health (only your own path's secret essence can be used). The lead acts at full strength, the second at half, the third at a third. **Each technique fills from its body part's actions**, the way a mark's bond fills (`player.gd _part_action`: landed strikes fill the hand, rolls through attacks the legs, hits survived the heart, parries the brow, one action hitting several foes the throat, backstabs and finishers the back; Water density and fill gear help as for marks). It's **full at 20, 28 or 36** (by how many kinds; `CHARGE`, the brow's gift cuts it a quarter): full, a cast spends nothing you carry. Cast it before it's full and the missing share comes out as up to **24 extra strain** (`EMPTY_STRAIN`). **Carried essence boosts a cast:** if you carry its essences **unbanked** (3, 6 or 8 by how many kinds, in proportion with the lead counting double; the path's essence from your unbanked flow), the cast spends them and hits **50% harder** (`BOOST`), with "boosted" over your head; with less than all of it, nothing is spent. It's automatic (nothing to hold, which is clearest with a thumb on a phone), and a switch on the techniques page ("Boost casts with carried essence: on/off") keeps your essence for banking instead. Fill is kept from floor to floor (as a mark's is). Every technique you carry, with what it does and the button that casts it, is on the climber screen's **Abilities** tab (hold Tab or the Climber button to peek), and the train's Abilities page offers a practice room to try them. You **carry six**, one on each mark button (Q E F C X V; LB + a face button; the cast buttons on a phone), cast like spells (a short wind-up). They're built at the **essence bench** in the Workshop car (below, under the tree) and named at **Your car → Techniques**, which shows the six slots, then the chosen one's card: its part and form, **Build at the bench** (to the Workshop, on that slot), its essence gems (shown only: the essence bench changes them; a slot the part's level doesn't allow yet says so), what it will do in plain words and any "echoes", how it fills and its strain, its boost and the boost switch, and an optional name; then ready-made recipes and the routing. A new Unbound starts with six ready-made ones (Ember Bolt, Gale Dash, Iron Will, Hunter's Mark, Tidal Shout, Stone Shell).

**Strain.** Each cast strains the technique's part: 14, 22 or 32 (by how many kinds). Above **70** its techniques hit 25% harder but may **waver** (35%: half strength, off aim). At **100** it **erupts**: you lose 45% of your health (**it can kill you**, a real fall), the part goes **dark for 8 seconds** (nothing from it can be cast) and raw essence shows on it. Strain fades (9 a second) once you haven't cast from that part for 1.5 seconds, and the stair's rest clears it. On the HUD each technique's medallion ring is its **fill** (bright when full, like a mark's) with its part's strain bar under it, a white tick at 70; on a phone the cast button's ring fills the same way and its colour runs hot with strain. A Vial of Essence fills techniques as it fills marks.

**How they look.** Instead of marks, **veins of raw essence** spread from each part on the climber, in the colour of the kind most pooled there, more and brighter with its level (with a soft glow over the darkness); strain makes them flicker hot, a dark part goes grey with raw essence circling it. The climber screen's figure shows the same veins, with each part's level, and a card per part (its raw density by kind, the next level and its gift).

**Dev tools** (the dev page's "The Unbound"): become Unbound or go back, show the refusal, on a first climb "Break a barrier" / "Unbreak the barrier" (whether the Regard will refuse you), add 60 raw density to a part, add 40 strain to a part. The Entity explains becoming Unbound, the refusal, the first barrier (the warning), fists, rules broken, a barrier that holds after the first climb, strain, an eruption, techniques and condensing the first time.

## The tree, the library and the book

How marked climbers and the Unbound see what they have and choose how they grow. Every choice is **for good**.

**The library** (`scripts/library.gd`): one shared set of pieces, in data (every name, line and number is in the Data editor: "The library, the tree and the book" in Words, "The library: forms, effects, variations, forks" in Numbers). **Forms** are the shapes body parts give (the eleven first ones: Strike, Bolt, Dash, Leap, Shell, Push, Shout, Pull, Mark, Sight, Within, and two more per part, all written: `unbound_forms.gd`); **effects** are what an essence does (a base one per essence, and three more per essence, all written: `unbound_effects.gd`); **variations** are small alterations shared by both paths: **Wide** (wider, a little weaker), **Long** (reaches farther), **Quick** (winds up in half the time, a little weaker), **Split** (three smaller copies fan out), **Lingering** (leaves a field for 3 seconds), **Chain** (jumps to one more foe), **Delayed** (a moment later, harder), **Mirrored** (also behind you), **Echo** (again, weaker, a second later), **Charged** (a longer wind-up, much harder). Each adds 4 strain to a technique. Every entry has an id, a name, one plain line, its numbers (and per-level changes) and its strain. A piece marked `"placeholder": true` isn't written yet: the tree shows it as **Coming later** and it can't be chosen, so the game works at every stage. The Unbound's techniques (`technique.gd`) read their shapes and kinds from the library and play exactly as before.

**Echoes:** certain mixes come close to a bond's spell, never the same (`library.gd ECHOES`, and ten more in `unbound_effects.gd ECHOES`: 16 across 14 bonds). A recipe is a form, the essence that leads and any it needs among the others: an Air shout with Stone echoes **Gale Lift**, an Air bolt with Iron **Piercing Shot**, a Water shell with Stone **Barrier**, a Water push **Tidal Surge**, a Fire bolt with Air **Fireball**, an Air strike with Fire **Chain Lightning**, a Water pull with Iron **Undertow**, a Stone leap with Iron **Quake**, a Fire strike with Blade **Kindle Blade**, a Blade bolt with Iron **Conjured Blades**, a Fire sight with Air **Smoke Veil**, an Iron mark with Stone **Challenge**, a Life within with Fire **Rekindle**, a Fire dash with Air **Wingburst**, a Water bolt with Blade **Venom Spit** and a Blade mark with Air **Track**. No ready-made technique echoes: they're found by experimenting. Each part's tree opens two new forms, at levels 3 and 7 (each adds strain on top of a technique's: 2 for a level-3 form, 4 for a level-7 one). **Hand:** **Volley** (four small bolts of 7 in a fan; they bend a little toward a foe ahead within 150, and each that strikes a foe the same volley already struck hits 30% harder for every earlier hit, so a volley on one foe adds up) and **Brand** (a touch marks the foe in front; 0.8 s later it bursts around it, or on the floor if no one was there). **Legs:** **Stride** (for 3 seconds you're quickened and leave a glowing trail; foes on it take the effect every half second) and **Vault** (a hop backwards, untouchable for 0.6 seconds, with a burst of 18 where you stood; the burst stays on the ground and bursts twice more, half a second apart, at 40%, each with the technique's effect). **Back:** **Wake** (a slow ring rolls out from you, striking each foe once and pushing it) and **Mantle** (a light shell and a cloak of light on your back; the next blow you take, on the shell or on you, is answered with a burst that throws foes off). **Throat:** **Hum** (a field around you that pulses three times, half a second apart, and moves with you) and **Call** (a burst far away where you aim, on the first foe near your aim if there is one). **Brow:** **Glare** (a narrow beam you hold for a second; it turns with your aim, stops at walls, damages every 0.2 s and brings the essences' effects every third tick) and **Omen** (a sign on the floor a step ahead that waits 10 seconds and bursts when a foe steps on it). **Heart:** **Heartbeat** (the effect beats out around you every second, four times) and **Wellspring** (a pool at your feet for 7 seconds; while you stand in it, it works on you as Within does, a tick at a time). Every hit goes through the technique's essences (lead, second, third, chosen effects), Air stretches each form's reach, and every variation works on each: Wide and Long widen and stretch it (Long also lengthens Stride, Mantle and Wellspring and widens the rings), Quick, Charged, Echo, Delayed, Chain and Lingering as on any technique, and a form centred on you (Wake, Hum, Heartbeat, Wellspring) under Split or Mirrored is cast a step away along each copy's aim, so Split fans three smaller copies out in front and Mirrored puts one behind you. **Capstones** (level 10, a part maxed; strong, but each plays through strain): **Raw Hands** (every hand technique also throws raw bolts at up to three foes near you, five while the hand runs hot), **Afterimage** (every legs technique leaves an afterimage where you stood that bursts a moment later, harder the hotter your legs run), **Unbowed** (every back technique also raises a light shell, and while any shell holds you, every part's strain fades, even as you cast), **Rallying Cry** (every throat technique fills your other techniques by a quarter, cools their parts by 10 strain and gives back your stamina), **Piercing Gaze** (every brow technique marks every foe near you for 4 seconds, 6 while the brow runs hot: they take 50% more damage) and **Second Heart** (once a floor, below a third of your health, 40% of it back and every part's strain gone). Their numbers are in each capstone's entry in `TREE`. The file runs them itself with one node under the game (`UnboundForms`, which also draws the forms' beams, rings, trails, signs, pools and cloaks in code); it hears each cast through `Library.on_cast`. Tests: `tests/parts/unbound_forms.gd`.

**The book** (`scripts/book.gd`): the climber screen is a bound grimoire, drawn in code: a leather cover tooled in gilt, an open spread of candlelit parchment (dark vellum, so the menus' light ink reads on it), the spine's crease, and a silk **ribbon** for each section (Abilities gold, Tree green, Marks crimson, Essence blue, Record violet, Core silver; colours in the Data editor's "The climber's book"). Pages turn with the ribbons, **Q** / **E**, **LB** / **RB** or a swipe across the page, and a soft shadow sweeps across as they do. It opens from your name on the rail, **Tab**, the **Climber** button, and **the book lying in Your Car** on the train (a thick volume on a cushion, beside the Inventory and Appearance; a gilt mote glows over it while a choice waits). It reflows at every Interface size like every page.

**The Tree section** (`scripts/tree_view.gd`, where all its drawing lives): the body map. Your climber stands on the left (the same figure as the Marks tab) with a **node on each body part**; a part with a ready fork pulses gold. Buds show what each piece is to you: **yours**, **in use until you choose** (today's side of a ready fork), a **ready choice** (pulsing gold), **locked** (a lock; the card says what opens it), **passed on** (faded, struck through), **learnable again** (violet: the Unbound) and **Coming later** (dashed). Select a bud for its **grimoire card** below, and **Choose ..., for good**: the first press only arms it ("Press again to choose Ball Lightning, for good"), the second chooses. The Entity explains that choices are for good the first time a fork is ready. The Unbound's passed sides have **Learn ... · N crystal essence**, also two presses. **The grimoire card** (`scripts/grimoire_card.gd`): one card for every entry, the same in Abilities, on the Tree and later at the essence bench: a small drawing (the medallion, or a glyph of what it does: a cone, a fan of bolts, a ring...), the name and a tag, the one plain line, a line of numbers, how it's cast (its key) and what it costs, an "echoes" line, and "+ more" for the full numbers.

**The art of the tree and the book** (all drawn in code: `tree_view.gd`, `book.gd`, `grimoire_card.gd`, the window's star in `train_view.gd`). **The body map:** the chosen part's tree grows out of the climber like a mark spreading: a branch in the bond's colour leaves the mark (little veins spreading round it), crosses to a **root** bearing the bond's emblem and runs down as a trunk, a node on it for each step (its number in a disc: filled once grown, gold and pulsing when a fork waits, dark while locked, dotted for what's to come), each option a **bud** on a twig, a fork's two sides on two twigs with "or" between. Buds: yours glow, passed-on ones fade and are struck through, a ready choice pulses gold, a locked one shows a lock and what opens it ("at step 4"), a passed Unbound side what learning it costs, "Coming later" is faint and dashed; each has the glyph its data suggests. Side by side on a computer and a phone at Normal; at a bigger Interface size the tree goes under the figure. **Reaching it:** tap a part, then a bud; with the keys or a controller, up and down the parts, **Right** onto the branch (on the chosen bud), up and down along it, Left and Right between a node's buds, Left off it back to the part, **A** or **Enter** to read a bud (and its card's Choose). **Links:** every Abilities entry has **On the body map** (the Tree opens with its bud chosen and glowing a moment), and a bud's card has **In your abilities** (back to that entry, picked out and scrolled to). **The window's star:** while a choice waits, a new star shines in a window of Your Car (the first spot clear of the car's things, the moon and the towers); tap or click it and the book opens on the Tree, on that part. A spell's glyph sits on a disc of its colour with its bond's emblem as a small seal. Tests: `tests/parts/art.gd`.

**Dev tools** (the dev page's "The tree"): how many pieces you own and choices are ready, **Forget every choice** (to try the forks again) and **Open the Tree**.

### Adding spells: the content contract

Several people can add spells at once without touching each other's files. Each tower has its own file, **`scripts/spells/<tower>.gd`** (gryphon, kraken, wyvern, manticore, firebird, chimera), and the Unbound have **`scripts/spells/unbound_forms.gd`** and **`scripts/spells/unbound_effects.gd`**. `library.gd` loads them all when it starts and merges what they declare; nothing else needs registering. The contract is also written at the top of `library.gd`.

**A tower's forks** (`static var FORKS := {bond_id: {...}}`), for each of its bonds:

```gdscript
"stormcaller": {
	"change": {"name": "Branching Storm", "says": "every leap splits in two..."},  # today's side of the step-3 choice, named
	2: {"id": "ball_lightning", "name": "Ball Lightning", "type": "ball_lightning", "learn": 2,
		"desc": "a slow orb of lightning drifts where you aim...",
		"steps": {2: {"cost": 10.0, "windup": 0.35, "damage": 6.0, ...}, 4: {"damage": 8.0}}},  # as in bonds.gd
	3: {"id": "lingering", "name": "Living Bolt", "desc": "...",
		"steps": {3: {"live": 3.0, "beat": 0.3, "live_power": 0.3, "live_hop": 220.0}}},  # replaces today's step-3 values
	4: {"id": "thunderclap", "name": "Thunderclap", "desc": "...", "placeholder": true, "learn": 4},  # not written yet
},
```

and the code that runs them:

- `static func cast_spell(bond, sp: Dictionary, p) -> bool`: runs your spell types (`sp["type"]`, unique in the game); true if handled. Use the bond's helpers as `bond.gd`'s own spells do: `bond.stat(key)`, `bond.damage()`, `bond.color()`, `bond.spell_power(sp)`, and the player `p` (`p.main`, `p.aim`, `p.global_position`).
- `static func cast_change(bond, sp, p) -> bool` (optional): your own way to run today's side of the step-3 choice from step 3 on (a Transformation's side A; its numbers are the first spell's step 3 in `bonds.gd`). Return false and the spell casts as before.
- `static func cast_variation(bond, sp, p, alt) -> bool` (optional): your own way to run a step-3 variation (call `bond._cast_type(p)` for the spell itself). Return false and the library runs it generically: Split, Mirrored, Echo and Delayed cast the spell again at their power; Wide, Long, Quick, Charged and Chain change its numbers (`library.gd mark_stat`).
- Things that stay a while (orbs, fields, timed casts) go through `scripts/spell_fx.gd`: `later(main, seconds, fn)`, `field(main, pos, radius, seconds, tick, col, fn)`, `orb(main, pos, vel, seconds, radius, col, fn)`, `mark(main, pos, seconds, col)`; and anything with its own drawing, `lasting(main, seconds, tick, paint, data)` (a small node: `tick(n, delta)`, `paint(n)` with `n.draw_*`, `n.fade()`, `n.finish()`) or `item(main, seconds, tick, paint, high)` (a dictionary painted into a `draw_util.gd` Batch). A new floor clears them all.
- **States and reactions** (`scripts/reactions.gd`; see **States, reactions and named pacts**): `Reactions.apply(main, foe, state, seconds, source, amount := 0.0)` gives a foe a state (`"charged"`, `"airborne"`, `"soaked"`, `"burning"`, `"poisoned"`, `"bleeding"`, `"slowed"`, `"unseen"`) or a moment (`"slam"`, `"drained"`) and returns the ids of the reactions it set off (`[]` if none); `amount` is burning or bleeding damage each half second or poison stacks (0: the state's own; -1: only the state, when your spell does its own damage). `Reactions.has(foe, state)`, `Reactions.left(foe, state)`, `Reactions.held(foe)`, `Reactions.clear(foe, state)`, `Reactions.num(id, key)`. Listen with `main.on_reaction(foe, id, source)` (or a foe's `on_reaction`), or `static func on_reaction(p, info)` in your file (`info`: id, foe, at, source, the states it held). A foe's old fields still work and still react: `e.charged = 4.0`, `e.ignite(dmg, s)`, `e.add_venom(n)`, `e.lift(s)`, `e.lose_track(s, null)`, `e.chill_t = s`, and the new `e.soak(s)`, `e.bleed(dps, s)`. A new state or reaction is a row in `STATES` or `REACTIONS` (and its effect in `reactions.gd _fire`). Give every spell the states it should leave, so reactions happen without anyone planning them.
- **Named pacts** (`scripts/pacts.gd`): `Pacts.has(p, id)`, `Pacts.held(p)`, `Pacts.num(id, key)`; a new pact is a row in `PACTS` (its two `bonds`, name, line, colour, numbers) and a `Pacts.has(...)` check where it acts. The file's header lists where each one acts.
- Hooks to listen to instead of watching numbers: `static func on_cast(p, cast)` in your file hears every cast (a technique's or a mark's spell: `Library.on_cast`); a foe's `on_hit(foe, dmg, source)` signal (and `main.on_hit` for any foe) when it loses health; the climber's `on_parry(source, perfect)`; `spell_fx.gd listen(node, signal, fn)` listens while your lasting node lives. To soften or harden a foe's blows, scale its `dmg_mult`, never `dmg`.

**New Unbound forms** (`unbound_forms.gd`: `static var FORMS`, as in `library.gd FORMS`, with `"part"`, `"class"`, `"says"`, numbers, `"strain"` and `"opens"` = the level) run in `static func cast_form(id, t, p, power, run, sh, w) -> int` (return the foes touched, -1 if it isn't yours; `sh` is the form's numbers with any variation applied, `w` the essences' weights; `Technique.hit(p, e, dmg, dir, w, power, run)` strikes a foe with every essence's effect, `run.add_bolt(...)` throws a bolt). Where a form opens is `TREE` in the same file. **New effects** (`unbound_effects.gd`: `static var EFFECTS`, with `"kind"`, `"name"`, `"says"`, a phrase for each class of form, numbers and `"opens"` = essence pooled) run in `static func hit_effect(id, p, e, share, power, run) -> bool` on a struck foe and `static func self_effect(id, p, share, power, run, cls) -> bool` on yourself (`cls` "self" for Within, "shell" for a Shell rising). Remove `"placeholder": true` once a piece is written. **Echoes** go in any file's `static var ECHOES`; what an echo adds in play is `unbound_effects.gd ECHO_ACTS` (by the spell's id).

**Tests** go in their own file under `tests/parts/` (a `run(t)` function; see `tests/parts/tree_foundation.gd`), so they don't collide either. `godot --headless -s tests/smoke_test.gd -- parts` runs only those.

## The climb

**How tall a tower is.** Every tower is 12 floors to the summit, where its beast waits (`SUMMIT` in `towers.gd`). Floor 6 is **the Proving**, where the tower takes your measure (see **The Proving** below). Your bondless first climb is 3 floors and ends in the Regard. Camps after floors 3 and 9 aren't floors.

**Floors are planned.** Floor 1 and the beast's floor are single open rooms. Every floor in between is planned on a grid of cells, one room each at most, and the grid grows with height: 4 by 3 cells on floors 2 to 5, 5 by 3 on floors 6 to 9, 5 by 4 on floors 10 and 11 (`PLAN` in `scripts/floors.gd`). Each floor has 6 to 11 rooms:
- **The spine** is the main route, a walk from cell to neighbouring cell. Each of its rooms has a job: the **entrance** (you start here), **guard rooms**, the **gallery** in the middle (it holds the floor's puzzle) and the **stair hall** (the stair stands at the top of it).
- **Branch rooms** (2 or 3) hang off the spine in the cells beside it: side rooms worth a look.
- A cell or two beside the spine is kept for a **sealed wing**, and sometimes one for a **secret room** (see **Sealed wings, vaults and secrets** below).

**Rooms.** A room is either **hand-made**, drawn as a small text grid in `scripts/rooms.gd` (one character per tile: walls, pillars, chasms, pools, monster spots and random tiles), or a shape **carved** in its tower's style: plain, open, a long hall, an L, a cross (corners notched), round (corners cut), pillared, a **ring** around one great block, a true **circle**, a long **gallery** with a row of pillars down each side, **split** on two levels (a ledge of low wall across it with steps through, the upper level lit a little) or strewn with **cover** to hide behind (`SHAPES` in `rooms.gd`; each tower's favourites are its `shapes` in `towers.gd`, and `scripts/floor_styles.gd` adds the newer shapes per tower: the hook for giving each tower its own layouts, corridor style and rules later). Hand-made rooms suit their job (calm start rooms, combat rooms, stair rooms, puzzle rooms), flip at random so they don't repeat exactly, and never appear twice on a floor. A wall that touches a room's edge becomes part of its outline; one standing free inside is a low wall you can hide behind (it blocks movement, sight and shots). The game checks that the stair, every monster, every piece of the floor's task, the chests and the cache can be reached (any low wall that would cut one off is left out). To add a room, paint it in **Dev tools → Paint rooms** (see The room painter), or copy one in `rooms.gd` and draw your own.

**Corridors and doors.** Corridors are two tiles wide and run straight from one room's wall to the facing wall of the next, as near the middle of both walls as the rooms' shapes allow, so they never cut through a room and what's drawn in it stays as drawn. Stone posts frame each doorway. A floor isn't a tree: one to three **loops** join rooms in neighbouring cells that weren't joined (never the start room or the stair room, which each keep their one way in) (`loops` in `floor_styles.gd`), and about half of them are doors **barred from one side**: a heavy wooden bar across them. From the barred side it stays shut ("Barred from the other side"); come round to its far side and the bar lifts: a shortcut back. Many doorways have a **door** (`scripts/doors.gd`): likelier on rooms where danger waits (three in four on dens, crypts, the stair landing, guard posts, strongrooms, scenario rooms and the puzzle room; one in three elsewhere; never on the start room). A closed door is solid: it blocks the way, shots and sight, so a room behind one stays dark until you open it. Walk up to a door and it swings open (a thud and a puff of dust); awake monsters open doors too. A door's look hints at the challenge inside, never the reward: plain planks for an ordinary room, iron bands, claw marks and bones at the threshold for a den, an engraved sigil of the hazard (wind, a wave, a flame, spikes, an ember, thorns or a chasm) for a trap room, a stone door with the puzzle's emblem (an eye or a plate) for the puzzle room, warm light under the door for a good room, and chains and a red glow for danger (a crypt, the stair landing), and a dark stone slab carved with a watching eye for a sealed wing.

**Room themes.** Every room has a **theme**: what it is (`scripts/themes.gd`, where the names, rules, tints and door looks live, all easy to rename). The first time you walk into a themed room its name and one-line rule show across the screen, for example "MONSTER DEN: Bones and claw marks. Something lives here." Each theme also washes the room's floor in its colour (with bones in a den, scorch marks in a furnace). Themes are given by rules, not pure chance: the start room is the **Arrival Hall**, the stair room the **Stair Landing** (a destination, lightly guarded: one guard, two from floor 6, and it never shuts you in) and the gallery takes its puzzle's theme (**Hall of Statues**, **Hall of Mirrors**, **Hall of Stones**, **Ice Hall**, **Essence Lock**, **Hall of Pedestals**, **Hall of Eyes** or **Weighing Room**), or the floor's task's hall (**Hall of Runes**, **Wardstone Hall**), or else a theme like any other room. Some rooms are fixed first: **scenario rooms** (see below), now and then a calm **Still Chamber** and, from floor 3, a **Wandering Trader**. Of the other rooms, the first one after the start is always a kind one (a good room) and the next a **Monster Den**; after that comes a mix, with no bad theme twice a floor, a cap on bad rooms (2 on floor 2, 3 up to floor 4, then 4), each tower's own themes twice as likely, and a theme that doesn't fit its room swapped for one that does.

**Every room pays off.** No room is empty and no dead end is pointless: each has a name and something for you. A good room has its gift; a danger room (a den, a crypt, a pillared hall, a trap room, the Chasm, a **Guard Post**) has a **chest** of spoils, shut fast while the monsters that wait in the room still stand and open once they're down (essence, sometimes an item or gear; richer in a dead end: `scripts/room_rewards.gd`, `RULES`); a room holding a piece of the floor's task has that; a **Still Chamber** (no monster comes there; stand in it and your wounds slowly close), a **Wandering Trader** (three items for unbanked essence; walk on if you'd rather not), a **Strongroom** behind a lock (a vault's cards on a pedestal), a scenario room, the puzzle's vault. The only empty hall left is the deliberate Still Chamber. The floor bot's sweep counts rooms with no payoff and dead ends with nothing, and both stay at zero.
- **Good rooms:** a **Healing Spring** (stand in the pool to heal until it runs dry), a **Forgotten Cache** (an urn of the tower's essence: walk onto it for a burst, more on higher floors; sometimes it holds gear), a **Fallen Climber's Camp** (a cold fire, a bedroll and an item left behind), and each tower's own (the Gryphon's Windswept Aerie, the Kraken's Tidepool, the Wyvern's Cold Forge, the Manticore's Hidden Oasis, the Firebird's Ember Pyre, the Chimera's Thornwild Nest). Two at most a floor, and no traps in them.
- **Bad rooms:** the **Monster Den** (more of the floor's monsters wait there), the **Restless Crypt** (from floor 3: its dead rise when you walk in), the **Pillared Hall** (archers like it), the **trap rooms**, each laying its own set piece of traps (see Traps: the Spike Gallery, Dart Hall, the Furnace and Miasma Hall in any tower, plus each tower's own: the Gryphon's Windhall, the Kraken's Sluice Hall, the Firebird's Emberfall and the Chimera's Bramble Hall; every floor from 2 has one), and in the Firebird's tower **the Chasm**, a band of chasm right across a room (half the time a narrow plank **bridge** crosses it: walk it, or jump anywhere else). Each keeps a chest of spoils. Where the dice would have left a room quiet, it's a **Guard Post** instead: guards and a strongbox.

Monsters wait in the rooms: most in the den, one or two guarding the stair, the rest along the route and in the branches (each dead end gets one before the rooms on the way through), never in the start room, a good room, a rest room, a trader's room or a scenario room (hand-made rooms' marked spots first). New kinds come in floor by floor: floor 2 has only the simplest (slimes and each tower's small ones), archers and each tower's middle monsters join on floor 3, knights and the heaviest (smiths, rams) on floor 4 (`from` in `MONSTERS`, `floors.gd`). A monster stays put until it sees you (or you come close, or it's hit), then rouses its neighbours and finds its way to you around the walls (a path grid, `AStarGrid2D`). Monsters can't attack what they can't see, and walls stop every shot. Each room has one torch on its north wall.

**Fog.** On floors of rooms you see only what your climber can see: walls block sight (chasms, water and pillars don't), places you've seen stay dimly mapped, and places you haven't are dark. Monsters out of sight are hidden until you see them (`scripts/fog.gd`), and closed doors block sight like walls. The cache's and spring's numbers live in `CACHE` and `SPRING` in `scripts/floors.gd`. In the Firebird's tower the rooms' chasm tiles are real chasms, and every floor has a chasm to jump (a Chasm room, or failing that a long corridor cut by one); elsewhere chasm tiles are walls. In the Kraken's tower the rooms' pools fill with water. An arrow at the edge of the screen points at the floor's task's next piece, then at the open stair.

**The floor's task.** Every floor of rooms (not floor 1, the camps, the Proving or the summit) has **one main task**, and its stair is **warded** (a chain across the steps) until it's done: it won't open, however many monsters fall. On arrival a banner names it and says how; the HUD keeps a short line at the top ("Braziers lit 2 of 4"); an arrow at the screen's edge points at the nearest piece still to do (and a gold chevron bobs over it once it's on screen), then at the stair. Its pieces are spread over several rooms, on the main route and the branches, never in the start room, so the task takes you round the floor. A climb never gets the same kind twice if it can be helped (`scripts/objectives.gd`, where every name, line, count and timing lives; `scripts/objective.gd` runs it):

| Task | What you do |
| --- | --- |
| **Light the Braziers** | Stand by each brazier (2, then 3, then 4 higher up) until it catches; a blow while you light one puts it out. |
| **Walk the Rune Path** | Read the tablet (in another room) to learn the order, then step on the runes in the Hall of Runes in that order (3, then 4). A wrong step wakes two guards and the path starts again. |
| **Raise the Levers** | Strike three levers up, in three rooms. Each falls back after a while (shorter higher up, but always long enough to walk the route), so all must be up at once. |
| **Carry the Sun Orb** | Carry the orb from the room farthest from the stair to the pedestal by the stair. A blow knocks it from your hands. |
| **Find the Keeper** | A Keeper (a crowned champion) and one or two guards wait in a far room. It carries the stair's key: picking the key up is the task. |
| **Wake the Wardstone** | Walk up to the stone in the Wardstone Hall to wake it, then keep the beast's creatures off it while it charges (they head for it); it only charges while none is near. |
| **Gather the Key Shards** | Three shards in three rooms, each with a guard. |
| **Catch the Motes** | Motes (4 to 6, named for the tower: wind, tide, ember, sand, spark, seed) drift away from you inside their rooms: corner them. |
| **A puzzle** | Any of the eight puzzles below can be the task: then the gallery holds it, and solving it lifts the ward. |

When it's done: **THE STAIR IS UNSEALED**, a column of gold light on the stair, a burst of essence, and a **reliquary** beside the stair: walk up to it and take one of three (a relic: a ring or an amulet; a piece of gear; an item). When the floor's puzzle isn't the task, the gallery may still hold one, for its own **vault** (a vault's cards for solving it); it never seals you in. The dev tools' **Puzzles** buttons make that puzzle every floor's task.

**Puzzles.** When the floor's task is a puzzle, it's in the **gallery**. It's all part of the Regard: the beast watches, and tests. A main puzzle never needs a bond, an item or a piece of gear, only what every climber has (strikes, a bow, rolls, jumps, parries, walking), and it can never lock you out. Each kind is **generated** at random and **checked by a solver** before it's used, so it's always solvable, and it gets harder up the climb: **tier 1** on floors 2-3, **tier 2** on 4-8, **tier 3** on 9-11. The rule text stands **in the world**, in a framed panel near the puzzle that the darkness doesn't dim (with "The beast is watching."). Eight kinds so far:

| Puzzle | How it's made and checked | How to solve it | Harder higher up |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Three Statues** ("The Riddling Statues" in the Manticore's tower) | Three statues before three plates, each saying something about where the way lies or about each other; a plaque gives the rules (one always tells the truth, the other two always lie). Each tower favours its own (the Gryphon mirrors, the Kraken the ice, the Wyvern the crumbling path, the Manticore the statues, the Firebird the pedestals, the Chimera the lock: half its floors).

**How puzzles meet combat.** Low down a puzzle is **quiet**: it sleeps ("The statues will not speak while blood is spilled") until the monsters in its room are dead. From floor 7 it's **pressure**: it wakes at once, and while you're in its room the beast sends a monster every few seconds (two at a time at most), so you think while you fight. Some puzzle rooms **seal** when you step in (the statues, the crumbling path and the lock from tier 2, the mirrors at tier 3; never the ice or the pedestals, whose way back is through the door): the doorways grind shut until the question is answered. A **wrong answer** (a wrong statue plate, a wrong spark, a pedestal out of order) brings an **ambush** and opens the seal so you can fight or run; once the ambush is dead (or after 40 seconds at most) the puzzle **resets**, the statues with a new riddle. It never stays shut.

**Bonds that break puzzles.** Some bonds give a **shortcut** (never needed; the panel says so when you have one): the Windwalker's **Crosswind** turns every mirror its wall of wind passes; the Hunter's **Keen Eyes** shows which statues lie after a few moments near them; the Flamewing's **Phoenix Glide** passes over the stones unfelt, so you can cross a lit one; the Stormcaller's **Static** makes a woken eye spark to the nearest sleeping one; the Sandshaper's **Rooted Stance** is heavy enough to hold a plate down.

Solving the floor's puzzle lifts the ward ("THE STAIR IS UNSEALED"), opens the stair (and a seal), and gives a burst of the tower's essence (behind a **gate** in the hand-made rooms). Floor 1 and the beast's floor have no ward: there the stair still opens once three quarters of the monsters are down. Each kind lives in its own script in `scripts/puzzles/` (`statues.gd`, `mirrors.gd`, `crumble.gd`, `ice.gd`, `lock.gd`, `memory.gd`, `eyes.gd`, `plates.gd`, each with its names, rule text and shortcuts as data at the top); `catalog.gd` picks how hard and which towers favour which; `scripts/puzzle.gd` is the host that runs one (the reward, the seal, quiet and pressure modes, failure and the ambush, auto-aim and the rule panels).

**Traps.** Traps are the beast's tests, laid **on purpose** and meant to be hard (`scripts/traps.gd`, where the names, timings, damage and placement rules live):
- **Trap rooms.** Every floor from floor 2 has at least one trap room (a room theme, see Room themes), and its traps are a **set piece built out of the room's own shape**: rows across its short side, slots in its two long walls, vents at its two ends. All the traps of a set piece keep **one shared beat**, so the pattern can be learned; higher up the beat quickens, the rows close up, and a second wave runs between the first (from floor 7). From floor 4 **monsters wait among the traps** (one, then two from floor 8), so you fight inside the pattern. The room's one-line rule only hints at how it works.
- **Traps may block the way.** A set piece can run right up to a doorway, and a spike patch spans a corridor on the way to the stair, so you must cross it.
- **Guards.** From floor 3, spikes ring the cache's urn two tiles out, rising one after another around the ring.
- **Corridors** hold one spike patch a floor (two from floor 7), all on one beat, some of its spikes hidden.
- **Hidden plates** (one to three a floor) can be anywhere but on a door itself.

**Hard to spot.** A trap at rest shows only a faint tell on its tile (a few small holes, a grate, cracks, a scorch ring, a slit in the wall), never its reach, and it's drawn under the darkness, so you only see it where your light or a torch falls. **Hidden** traps show nothing at all until you're right on top of them. The Hunter's **Keen Eyes** shows every trap, outlining the hidden ones in gold; the dev tools' **Show traps** outlines every trap and its reach.

**Short warnings.** Just before it fires a trap gives a short tell, never a flash: a click or a hiss, spikes peeking out of their holes, a grate shimmering, a slit's eye reddening, a wisp of gas, a ring warming. Grates, slits and rings also give a small glint in the last moment (0.25 s); spikes, snares and gas don't. A hidden plate, once stepped on, gives you even less time (0.3 s) to get clear. Only fire and falling embers light the dark. Traps **hurt badly** (about 10 to 28, more higher up). Rolling through a firing trap counts as a dodge (feeding your legs), but only once per trap. Monsters that wander into one are caught too. The Manticore's and Chimera's towers also hide **gas vents**: set one off and a cloud spreads that keeps hurting while you stand in it; it can kill you if you don't get out.

**Never shut for good.** After the traps are laid, a **protection pass** keeps them off the start, the stair, the doors themselves, the puzzle room and the good rooms (only the cache's guard ring goes there), then searches the floor treating as walls only the tiles that are **never** safe (where overlapping beats leave no moment to cross). While the stair, the puzzle room, a healing spring or a camp can't be reached that way, it opens one such tile on the way (cutting a lane short there, or taking a trap away) and searches again. So the way through may be guarded, but a climber who times it, rolls or jumps can always get through. A chasm doesn't count: it's jumped. Monsters never wait on a trap tile, and nothing that matters ever sits in a chasm. The dev tools' **Speed readout** shows, at the bottom right, the frame rate and frame time, draw calls, node count, and how many monsters, traps and lights there are (to check for lag on a phone).

**Cheap to draw.** A trap at rest never changes, so every resting trap (and a set piece's path and brambles) is drawn once into a few squares of the floor; each frame only the traps warning or firing on screen (and hidden ones right beside you) are drawn, all in one batch, 30 times a second.

**The stair.** On floor 1 and the beast's floor the **stair** at the top of the room opens once three quarters of the monsters are down; on floors of rooms it opens when the floor's task is done. Walk onto it to climb, or finish the rest first for their essence (the HUD shows how many are left). Taking the stair is a rest: burns heal, you recover a third of your health and all your stamina. Each floor's monsters are bought from a budget that grows with height, and they get tougher too (`CURVE` and `MONSTERS` in `scripts/floors.gd`, both in the Data editor's Monsters section). Falling ends the run and sends you back to floor 1. Your best floor is saved.

## Scenario rooms

Some rooms are short set pieces you play through, and how you do is **noticed** (`scripts/scenarios.gd`, where every number and word lives; their names and rules are the `scn_` rooms in `themes.gd`). Most floors from 2 have one (in a dead end first), and higher floors sometimes two. Each waits on a glowing gilt **mark** in its room: nothing starts until you step onto it, so you can walk past. Six shared kinds, and the towers' own:
- **The Last Camp:** a fallen climber's camp is still burning. Keep the monsters off it for 30 seconds: they go for the fire unless you come close.
- **The Falling Hall:** the roof comes down. Run for the far end, watching the rings where rocks will land.
- **The Champion's Circle** (from floor 3): the doors shut and a champion of the tower fights you, fairly and alone.
- **The Watchful Hall:** lanterns sweep the hall. Cross to the far end without their light falling on you; walls and cover hide you, and each time you're seen a monster comes.
- **The Caged Cub** (from floor 3): a small grey creature whimpers in a cage. Break it open, then lead it to the door while the monsters go for it.
- **The Gauntlet:** the doors shut; beat every wave, and be quick.
- **Hold the Forge** (the Wyvern's tower, from floor 3) and **the Sinking Court** (the Manticore's tower): the towers' own scenarios, described with their towers above. A tower's own scenario turns up only in its tower, three times as often as one of the shared ones (`towers` and `weight` in `SCENARIOS`), and its tower's floor script runs it.

Each ends with a **grade**: gold, silver, bronze or none (a fall, leaving the room, the camp or the cub lost). A grade pays essence and **renown** (`Renown.earn`), feeds the Makers' interest (`Makers.feed(main, "scenario")`), and goes on the climb's tally of what the beast noticed (`main.noticed`, with `main.beast_noticed_count()`, for the summit or the Regard to read later), with a banner: **THE GRYPHON NOTICED**. Their status shows under the task's line at the top of the screen.

## The Proving (floor 6)

Halfway up every tower (never on the bondless first climb), floor 6 is a special test that shows what the tower is about. It has its own fixed layout instead of planned rooms (`scripts/proving.gd`, where all its words and numbers live; each tower's picks are `gauntlet`, `sentinel` and `sentinel_adds` in `towers.gd`; "the Proving" is a placeholder name):

1. **The antechamber**, where you arrive, with two doors in its north wall and their words under them. **The Proving** (a gold six-pointed star) opens when you walk up to it. **The Lethal Proving** (the star in red, cracked, red light under it) stays sealed until you have won the Proving in that tower once; then bumping it explains it, and you **lean on it** for a moment to go in. Choosing one bars the other.
2. **The gauntlet**: the tower's own trap room (the Gryphon's Windhall, the Kraken's Sluice Hall, the Wyvern's Furnace, the Manticore's Spike Gallery, the Firebird's Emberfall, the Chimera's Bramble Hall). The way behind you shuts. Like every trap room it can always be crossed by timing: no tile on the way is never safe.
3. **The sentinel** (a door bearing a crown): a champion of the tower's sentinel (thunderling, shellback, smith, ram, wisp, mongrel) with two to four of its adds. The doors shut until they fall; the champion leaves an essence crystal.
4. **The beast's shadow** (a dark door with a pale eye, violet light under it): an echo of the tower's beast, drawn in faded violet with wisps rising off it, with about a third of its summit health; it calls no help and the Firebird's doesn't rise again. Its bar says "Shadow of the ...". The doors shut until it falls; its fall doesn't count as slaying the beast.
5. **The reliquary**, sealed until the shadow falls: a pedestal offers **one of three relics** (rings and amulets, Rare on the first win), never shown before you win. Then the stair.

**Losing isn't death.** In the ordinary Proving, when your health would reach 0 the tower "has taken your measure": you are **cast down to a fresh floor 5**, keeping half your unbanked essence, at half health, with a **lingering wound** (a quarter less max health, shown violet on the health bar with a small "wounded", and on the climber screen) that closes after two stairs, when you reach floor 7. Climb back and face floor 6 again. Only the **Lethal Proving**, chosen knowingly, ends the climb if you die there.

**Harder on later climbs.** Every win is saved per tower (`provings` in the save) and raises that tower's Proving a tier, up to 3: the gauntlet is laid as a higher floor's (faster, denser), the sentinel is tougher with more adds, the shadow stronger, and the relics are tempered, some of them Storied. The Lethal Proving is a tier above that. (Everywhere else difficulty rises only with the floor.) The tiers are `TIERS` in `proving.gd`.

## Locked doors and lock-in rooms

**Locked doors** (from floor 3, most floors have one, some higher floors two; `LOCKS` in `floors.gd`, the words in `LOCK_TEXT` in `doors.gd`). A lock never stands on the way to the stair or to any piece of the floor's task: only a **Strongroom** (a branch room that holds nothing the floor needs, made into a vault: a pedestal with a vault's cards, lightly guarded) or a sealed wing (which holds a vault already) is locked, so what's behind a lock is always worth it. What opens it is always found on the main route, so you can reach it without passing the lock. A locked door keeps its usual look with a lock drawn over it; bump it and it stays shut with a clank, "Locked", and a line saying how it opens. Monsters can't open it either. Keys that are part of the floor's task (the Keeper's key, the shards) aren't door keys: picking them up is the task.
- **A padlock:** its key lies in another room (a small gold glint; walk over it), or **one of the floor's monsters carries it** and drops it when it falls. Walk up to the door with the key and it turns.
- **Iron bars:** a **lever** stands in another room; strike it and the bars lift, with a clank, a nudge of the camera and a banner.
- **A violet ward:** solving the floor's puzzle opens it.

**Lock-in rooms.** Some rooms shut their doorways behind you once you're well inside: monster dens (3 in 4), restless crypts (9 in 10), some guard rooms (3 in 10), the stair landing from floor 6 (3 in 5), and the sealed wings (`LOCK_IN` in `doors.gd`). Iron bars drop with a slam and a banner ("THE DOORS GRIND SHUT"), and the top of the screen says the doors are shut and how many foes are left (on a phone too). They open again when the monsters that were in the room are dead. A room with no monsters left never shuts, and two and a half minutes open it whatever happens, so you're never stranded.

**There is always a way out.** `scripts/reach.gd` walks a floor the way the rules allow: a closed door opens as you walk up to it, a locked door only once its key, lever or carrier (or, for a warded door, every place the floor's task asks you to reach) can be reached without passing it, a door barred from one side once its far side is reached, cracked walls and what's behind them count as rock (they're never needed), and doorways that open again by themselves (a lock-in room's bars, a wing's gate, a puzzle's seal) don't count as walls. Every floor plan is checked with it before it's used (`Floors.rooms_floor`): the stair and every piece of the task, every room, each wing's door, and every key, lever and carrier must be reachable from the start, or the floor is planned again (the planner itself never makes such a floor that the tests have found: this is the backstop). A monster carries at most one key. The cover in rooms never walls off a piece of the task, a chest, a key, lever, mural, camp or spring (`Floors.cover_targets`), and traps keep clear of them. And while the climb goes on, every two seconds while a door is locked, the game checks from where you stand (`main.gd` `check_stuck`): if the stair can't be reached and a locked door in your way has no key, lever or carrier you could reach, that lock gives way, with a banner ("A LOCK GIVES WAY"; the words are `relent_title` and `relent` in `LOCK_TEXT` in `doors.gd`). The recall scroll isn't relied on for this (you carry one at most, not always, and not on the first climb).

**Doors that only look dangerous.** The stair landing and a restless crypt have a **chained** door (two chains across it, an iron ring where they cross, red light under it): danger waits there, but the door isn't locked and opens as you walk up. Only a locked door shows a gold padlock.

## Sealed wings, vaults and secrets

Everything here is part of the Regard: the beast watches and tests, using what comes into the tower. Names, lines and numbers live in `scripts/wings.gd` (`TEXT`, `CONDITIONS`, `WAVES`, `REWARD`, `SECRET`) and `scripts/items.gd` (`RECALL`, `CRYSTAL`), so they're easy to change.

**Sealed wings** (from floor 2; one a floor, two from floor 10). A room of 9-11 by 7-9 tiles is carved into each kept cell beside the spine, joined by a corridor to its spine room, behind a **sealed door** (a dark stone slab carved with a watching eye, red light under it). Cut into one of its walls is a 3x3 **vault alcove** behind a one-tile vault door drawn as a **pulsing gold seal with a keyhole**, with warm light leaking out from under it, so you know from the moment you see it that a locked vault waits and that the room's question opens it. The seal's emblem shows the **challenge**, never the reward: a shield (take no hit), an hourglass (be quick), crossed blades (staggered kills), or a puzzle's emblem.
- **Walk in and the way shuts behind you:** the doorway grinds closed (solid to walking, sight and monsters' paths) with a slam and a banner: "The way behind you grinds shut. This room will not let you go until its question is answered."
- **The question**, for now, is a **challenge room**: two or three waves of the tower's own monsters and one rule, shown on screen with the wave and the clock: **Untouched** (take no hit), **Swift** (beat every wave within the time given) or, from floor 4, **Staggered** (every foe must fall while staggered: parry it, then strike).
- **Keep the rule** and beat the waves: the seal breaks, the vault opens, and so does the way out.
- **Break the rule** and either the waves go on and the vault opens with a **lesser prize** at the end, or the tower sends its **champion**: a bigger, tougher version of one of its monsters (three times the health, crowned, a gold aura, and a ground slam telegraphed by a closing ring: roll out of it). Its fall reopens the way, but the vault's seal goes dark for good; the champion leaves an essence crystal where it fell.
- **You're never trapped:** every ending reopens the way out, and a question that drags on for four minutes gives up.
- **For puzzles later:** a puzzle host can claim a wing (its slot is `wing["puzzle_slot"]`): `puzzle.gd` answers `wing_puzzle(wing)` with a puzzle type (and `wing_emblem(type)` with the seal's emblem), gets `start_wing_puzzle(wing)` when the climber walks in, and calls `main.wings.wing_solved(id)` or `main.wings.wing_failed(id)` (a failure sends the champion).

**Vault rewards** are real things to walk up to: a **pedestal** in the alcove with a gold light over it. Walk up and choose one of its cards (like a camp's: keys 1 to 3, arrows and Enter, the d-pad and A, click or tap): two **essence crystals** of different kinds (the tower's own and another: a slight steer toward the next bond), and a piece of **gear** (the rarer of two), a **recall scroll** (only if you don't already carry one, and not on the first climb) or an **item**. A lesser prize is a smaller crystal and an item. The monsters wait while you choose.

**Essence crystals** hold essence outside you and are **kept between climbs** (in your save). Crystals of a kind combine into one. Consume some from the **Inventory** (I, or the Bag button on a phone: the Essence crystals row, then **Consume 10**, which banks up to 10 at a time) to bank that essence into you at once, as at a stair: your marks grow, and it counts toward which bond your next mark becomes. Or spend it at the train's store or bench (see **The train**).

**The recall scroll** (you carry one at most) shows with your items: make it your quick item (T, or the small arrow on a phone) and use it, or pick it in the Inventory and press **Read it**. It asks first; use it again within 3 seconds to read it. Reading it **leaves the tower safely, keeping everything**: your unbanked essence settles into essence crystals (one per kind) and this floor's soak is banked; there's no heirloom choice (the climb wasn't won); your pack comes with you, and you go on to the train. Its words won't come on the bondless first climb.

**Secret rooms.** Sometimes a kept cell holds a small room joined by a passage one tile wide whose first tile, in a spine room's wall, looks like any other wall (no torch hangs on it, and no trap or shelter pillar stands in front of it). Until it opens, the passage and room are solid rock to sight, fog and paths. Its hints: **dust** drifting down near it now and then when you're close, a **hollow knock** when you strike it, and **Hunter's Keen Eyes** outline it. **Lean into it** (walk into it) for three quarters of a second and it grinds open; on a phone, push the stick into it. Inside: a hidden **essence cache** (more than a good room's, sometimes with gear), a choice of three **essence crystals**, or a **Tithe Door**: a pale door with an open hand carved on it, asking for unbanked essence (it says how much, never what it keeps). Lean on it to pay; the vault behind it holds a full vault's cards.

**Cracked walls.** One or two walls a floor show **cracks** (drawn in code in the tower's stone, with chips at their foot). A finishing blow (the last strike of a weapon's combo) breaks one with a crumble of dust and rubble, and so does a burst: a Fire Flask, a Lodestone Bomb, an exploding spell. A lighter blow only knocks hollow ("A finishing blow might break it"), and dust drifts down from one when you're near. Behind it is always something: a **passage** between two rooms that weren't joined (a loop), or an **alcove** with an essence cache (sometimes gear) or a hidden vault's cards; some alcoves sit at the end of a short **corridor that ends at the cracked wall**, the clue. Cracked walls are never needed for the stair or the floor's task; the hidden wall you lean on (above) stays the rarer, subtler secret (`scripts/cracks.gd`, `TEXT` and `RULES`; how many and what they hide per tower: `cracks`, `crack_kinds` and `alcove` in `floor_styles.gd`).

## The Makers and their fragments

Three godlike beings, siblings of the Entity, watch every climb from outside the towers: **the Maker of Marks** (made the bonds; cold blue, an eye), **the Maker of Towers** (made the towers; amber, a trowel) and **the Maker of Creatures** (made the monsters; rose, a paw). The names are placeholders. **Their words are written** (the owner approved the first draft): what each Maker says, its eight fragments and the Entity's line about it, all in `scripts/makers.gd` (`MAKERS`), each in its own voice (Marks precise and cold, Towers proud and slow, Creatures warm and fierce). Any line can still be redacted: a string of `▇` shows as blacked-out word-shaped bars. Names, colours, signs, every number and the answers live there too.

- **Fragments** (their traces) are rare: at most one on a floor, on about one floor in eight (`FRAGMENT`), never on the first climb, and only on the north wall of a **secret room** or a sealed wing's **vault**, never on the way to the stair. A fragment is a pale inscription with its Maker's sign, faintly lit in its colour. **Walk up to it** to read it: the room's light shifts in the Maker's colour, its words float up for a moment, a banner counts how many you've found, and the Entity goes quiet for a while.
- **The forbidden shelf**: once you've found a Maker's first fragment (or met it), the **Archive** (the Library) gains its shelf (on the train, the forbidden volume on the Library's bookcase): its sign, how many fragments, each one (where you found it, and its words), and how your meetings went. Before anything is found, nothing shows. Once you've met one, the Entity speaks of each sibling there.
- **How they watch**: each Maker keeps an interest score across every climb, fed by what it cares about (`WEIGHTS`): the Maker of Marks by spells cast (more for spells not cast yet this climb) and bond steps grown; the Maker of Towers by traps rolled through, secret walls leaned open, locked doors opened, vaults opened and puzzles solved; the Maker of Creatures by kills, champions and beasts; all three by marks taken, wounds, Life or Unmaking banked (once those exist) and fragments read (most for a fragment's own Maker).
- **Glimpses**: as a Maker's interest grows (past about a third of what it needs), now and then after a stair: a second, far too long shadow beside you, the light bending for a moment, or the Entity falling silent.
- **The meeting**: a Maker shows itself only once you've found **at least six fragments** in all, and then only when its interest crosses its threshold (`REVEAL`). It comes in the quiet just after a stair: the game pauses and a vast presence in its colour fills the screen, an eclipse with lit slits for eyes, too big to fit, a small climber beneath it. It speaks, then you choose an answer on cards (left and right and Enter or A, the number keys, a click or a tap). The answers are "To understand it.", "To take more of it.", "To change it." and "Nothing. Only to climb."; which ones each Maker takes well is in its `good` list. A **good answer grants its secret bond** (the Developer, the Designer or the Manipulator) as a **core mark** (saved as the profile's `core_mark` and `core_depth`); a **bad answer turns it away**: its interest drops, it needs more next time, and it remembers. Once you have a Life or Unmaking path, there's also a card to **plead for a change of path**: it may refuse; if it agrees, the path is reset. Carrying a full vessel adds **Offer the vessel**: the same plea, likelier granted, and the Maker keeps the vessel (see **The vessel**).
- **The core mark**: a fine pattern over all six body parts at once, in its Maker's colour, beneath your tower marks: threads (Marks), stepped courses like masonry (Towers) or curving veins (Creatures). It shows on the climber in play and on the climber screen, which also names it. Each depth makes it fuller; its tools are described under "The secret bonds" above. **One secret bond per climber**: the other two can still show themselves, but the Maker whose core mark you carry may **interrupt** (half the time): it arrives mid-scene and cuts the meeting short, speaks over its sibling, or stays and listens, and no second bond is granted.
- **Hooks for the secret essences**: when a Maker grants its bond it gives secret essence (Creatures: Life, Towers: Unmaking, Marks: both) through `main.gain_secret(kind, amount)` (or `main.rise_secret`) if the game has it, and a granted plea calls `main.reset_path()` if it exists; otherwise nothing happens.
- **Dev tools** (the Makers section): add a fragment, add interest to a Maker, meet a Maker now (once the menu closes), a glimpse now, place a fragment on this floor, and clear everything.

## Mirror images

Part of the Regard too: the beast tests you with your own likeness. Every word and number is in `scripts/mirror.gd` (`ROOM`, `STATS`, `SWING`, `SHOT`, `ROLL`, `GUARD`, `SPELL`, `SHAPES`, `BOND_SHAPE`, `PRIZE`, `LOOK`, `TEXT`).

- **Mirror rooms.** From floor 4, about one floor in six (`ROOM`) turns one of its branch rooms (off the way to the stair) into a **Hall of Glass** (chosen before the other rooms get their themes, so the floor still has its good room, den and trap room): silvered mirrors stand along its walls and polished panes lie in its floor. Never on the bondless first climb, the Proving's floor (6) or the summit. Its doorway always has a door, bearing an oval of silvered glass with a hooded figure in it and cold light under it (it hints at the fight, never the prize). No ordinary monsters wait inside.
- **Walk in** and a mirror image of you steps out of the glass in the middle of the room, and the doors grind shut (a lock-in room, `LOCK_IN` in `doors.gd`) until it falls (the usual failsafe opens them after 150 seconds).
- **It is you as you walked in:** drawn like the climber (the same hood or helm, armor, gloves and boots, its weapon in the *other* hand) but in silvered glass, a shimmer sweeping across it, a faint cool double a pixel aside and a pale rim and glints at its feet so it reads in the dark. It carries the **weapon in your hand** (its swing chain, up to three links, with your weapon's reach and arcs; or your bow or crossbow), wears **your armor** (your blows on it are cut by your own protection) and bears **your marks**. Up to two of your bonds (the highest steps) give it a simpler spell named after the bond's first spell, by `BOND_SHAPE`: a **volley** of three bolts (Hunter, Blade Forger, Venomist (poisons), Covenant), a **blast** circle where you stand that locks half a second before it bursts (Stormcaller, Deepcaller, Rune Artist, Sandshaper, Flame Contractor), a **cone** that throws you back (Windwalker, Tidecaller, Flameblade, Grafter, Surgeon) or a **nova** ring around it (Warden, Ashwalker, Flamewing, Rekindler). Blasts, cones and novas can't be parried: roll out.
- **Fair and readable:** every attack has its wind-up and telegraph (its weapon wound back and a red cone; a red aim line that thickens as it locks; the spell's red circle, cone, ring or fan with an inner edge in the bond's colour) and glints a quarter second before it lands, then recovers, open (the gold ring). It sometimes **rolls** clear of a blow from the front (at most every 3 seconds), and after an attack it may raise its **guard** (a silver arc in front): a light blow from the front is turned aside, throwing you back and opening you for a moment while it answers with a swing (still telegraphed). A finisher, a blow from behind, a riposte while it's staggered or a lifted foe get through.
- **Health:** 140 at floor 1, growing with the floor like other monsters (184 on floor 5): a few monsters' worth, below a champion or a beast. Its swings deal your weapon's damage times 1.3; its arrows 75% of a full draw, slower than yours.
- **Its prize:** it is a **great foe**: its secret essence (mostly Unmaking, some Life, `secret.gd`) rises through the usual path, so the first one opens the path choice if you haven't chosen. It also leaves an **essence crystal** of the tower's kind worth half a vault's (like a champion's), and a 1 in 4 chance of a piece of gear a rarity row higher than the floor's. It can't be bound by Covenant.
- The Entity explains it the first time you meet one. The **dev tools** have a *Mirror images* row: spawn one near you, or make the next floor hold a mirror room.

## What's in this build

- **The base climber.** Started very clumsy; now a bit tougher: 140 health, quicker on its feet, faster stamina recovery, cheaper rolls, a slightly wider parry window, and a longer grace period after being hit so a crowd can't chain-hit you.
- **Eighteen bonds**, three for each of the six towers, all written (each a Body, a Passive and its spells, with every fork on its tree: see the towers' bonds above and Marks' trees below).
- **The Unbound**, the other path: techniques built at the essence bench, a tree per body part with its new forms, every effect and a capstone at level 10.
- **Overdraw.** Cast without enough essence and the spell still fires, but the body part burns: a burned hand weakens your strikes, burned legs make rolls cost more and travel less. Burns heal at the next stair.
- **Marks and motes on the body.** Each bond shows as a sigil that grows lines with its step, circled by motes as its essence fills.
- **Gear.** Ten melee weapons (canes among them), two bows, a crossbow and two pistols, four suits of armor, and twenty-five Common worn pieces plus Fine and Rare rings, amulets, packs and a bandolier for the helm, gloves, legs, boots, cloak, amulet, ring and backpack slots; Fine, Rare and Storied finds with twenty essence traits (never one twice on a piece), twenty-four named pieces (eight only in their own tower), six Regarded pieces left by the beasts, caps on what gear adds, wear on Common pieces and a 50% protection cap (see **Gear: weapons, armor and worn pieces** above). *Bows*: hold to draw (you walk slowly while drawing and stamina stops recovering), release to loose. Damage and speed grow with the draw, and a full draw (a soft tick and ring tell you) pierces through foes. A quick tap fires a weak shot; rolling or getting hit drops the arrow. Weapons are plain data in `scripts/weapons.gd`, armor and worn pieces in `scripts/gear.gd`.
- **Solid bodies.** The climber and monsters can't share space; overlapping bodies are pushed apart, and heavier ones (the knight) give way less. Foes lifted by Gale Lift pass over everyone.
- **Timing over mashing.** Swings cost real stamina (a sword chain is 58), and stamina waits almost a second after you attack before it refills, so mashing leaves you winded. Monsters have **poise**: once they commit to an attack, light hits don't stop it (read the glint, then parry or roll); only heavy hits (finishers) rock them, and light hits make a monster flinch at most once every 1.2 seconds, so you can't stun-lock it. After every attack a monster is **open** for most of its recovery: a gold ring pulses around it and your hits deal 60% more ("Opening!"). Beasts have less health than before, longer openings after their attacks, and their fire burns out faster.
- **Readable fights.** Every monster attack follows one rhythm: a wind-up, then a **glint** (a white star and a "ting") exactly 0.25 s before the blow lands, then a recovery where the monster is open. Parry on the glint and it always works; parry a beat after the glint for a **perfect parry** (longer stagger, more stamina back, bigger flash). The parry window is about a third of a second, turns you toward the attacker (or an incoming arrow) so aiming can't make it miss, and can cancel the tail of a swing. Only two monsters attack at a time; the rest circle and wait their turn. Slimes and archers flinch when hit (so aggressive play works on them); knights keep winding up through your hits, so parry, riposte or roll against them. The knight slashes up close and lunges from further out; the slime creeps up and pounces (touching it is harmless).
- **Combat feel.** Strikes chain into a 3-hit combo that costs stamina, with the third swing a heavy finisher; each swing lunges a little and locks your aim. Presses made up to 0.2 s early are buffered. Every hit gets a short hit-pause, sparks, knockback and a squash, and heavier hits get more of each. Hitting a staggered foe is a **riposte** for 2.5x damage. A clean **parry** flashes, rings, refunds some stamina and **reflects arrows** back at monsters. Rolls leave afterimages for exactly as long as you're invulnerable, and a landed dodge shows a ring. Getting hurt knocks you back, flashes the screen edge red and interrupts your swing or cast.
- **The look.** A dark tower: flagstone floor with moss and cracks, a brick north wall, flickering torches, drifting dust and embers. The darkness is **one layer** over the world (`scripts/lighting.gd`, like the web version's): a single screen-sized shader that darkens everything in the tower's own fog colour and cuts soft holes of light around the climber, the torches, the stair, the cache, lit puzzle eyes, burning floor and short flashes, and adds a vignette. It costs one draw call however many lights there are (up to 48 at once), and it reads the line-of-sight fog in the same pass. The open stair, the cache, lit eyes and the ward also get a soft coloured glow so the eye goes to them.

  **Shadows, spotlights and glow** (all in `lighting.gd`, built for the browser's WebGL2 and a phone, no 2D light nodes):
  - **Soft shadows.** Light stops at walls, closed doors, low cover and pillars, and fades softly around corners and doorways instead of shining through rock. Each light's view of the walls is worked out once on the CPU, on a grid of half tiles around it (ring by ring outward, each cell taking light from the cells nearer the light, so a shadow starts crisp at a wall's edge and widens into a soft penumbra), and kept as a 48x48 square in one small atlas texture that the darkness shader reads with smooth filtering. Torches, the stair and flashes never need it again; a moving light gets a new one a few times a second; a door opening redoes only the squares around it. A frame spends at most a millisecond or two on new shadows (`QUALITY` `cast_ms`). On High the climber's own light casts shadows too, worked out twenty times a second as you move, so pillars sweep their shadows round you (walls need none: the fog already hides what's behind them). A wall's brick face catches the light up to its top.
  - **Spotlights.** A light can shine in a soft-edged cone (`add_spot(pos, dir, cone_deg, reach, colour, ...)`, or `set_spot` on an existing light): the light under a lit door (a good room's warm glow, a chained or sealed door's red, the shade's violet) fans out into the corridor (`doors.gd` `UNDER_LIGHT`), and a vault's seal spills its gold out in front of it.
  - **Glow.** Bright things get a soft additive halo just above the darkness: torch flames, the open stair, the ward, the cache, the campfire, burning floor, spells' flashes, essence motes flying to the climber, and any registered light with enough glow (`BLOOM`). Halos of things out of sight fade with the fog. All of them are one more draw call.
  - **Settings → Lighting:** **Low** is the plain darkness (no shadows, no glow: the lightest for an old phone), **Medium** has shadows (worked out a little more slowly) and glow, **High** adds softer shadow edges and quicker updates for moving lights. A computer starts on High; a browser or a touchscreen (the phone build) starts on Medium. It's saved with the other settings.
  - Every number is in `QUALITY`, `SHADOW_*`, `SPOT_*` and `BLOOM` at the top of `scripts/lighting.gd`. Each tower's darkness colour and stone hue live in `scripts/palette.gd` (`TOWER_FOG`, `TOWER_TINT`). Characters are outlined and shaded: a hooded climber with a visible weapon, a wobbling slime whose eyes follow you, a hooded archer with bow and quiver, and an armoured knight with shield and plume who raises his sword to wind up. Telegraphs, swing trails, health bars and words in the world (damage numbers, gear names) are drawn above the darkness so they always read. The camera follows the climber, zoomed in. Spells, parries, crits and burns flash light (`main.light_flash`); slain monsters burst into debris.
- **Sound.** Recorded CC0 effects, layered by `tools/build_audio.py` (sword hits are a chop plus a punch body plus a low thump, parries are a metal clang with a ringing tail). Every weapon sounds like itself: a dagger's quick swish and slice, a spear's thrust, the dull whoosh and bone-deep thud of a mace or war hammer, and its own sound when you switch to it. Bows are real bow recordings: the stave creaks while you draw (lower on the longbow), the nock clicks at full draw, and the release is a string snap and a whoosh that grows louder and sharper the longer you held it; arrows land with a wooden thunk (into walls too). The crossbow fires with a hard clack, ratchets as it reloads and clicks when the bolt drops in. Hits sound different on each monster (armour clanks, slime squelches), the archer's bow creaks before it fires and the knight's armour rattles as he winds up, beasts' blows land with a deep slam, chain mail and plate jingle as you walk, trap plates click before they fire, gas vents hiss (`hiss`), and there are pick-up, armor and heirloom sounds, soft page-turn sounds in the menus, and a gong when the room is clear. Levels are set by kind (footsteps and menus quiet, hits in the middle, slams and parries loudest) so nothing jumps out, and in a busy fight one sound never plays more than four times at once. Looping music and tower ambience play underneath; M toggles them. The knight's wind-up and the archer's aim lock each play a warning tone. Every monster has a voice: a warning as it winds up (the bat's squeak, the thornhound's snarl, the scorpling's chitter, the ram's snort, the cinder's flare, the smith's grunt...), a cry when struck (spaced out so a flurry of blows doesn't make a choir) and its own death. Each beast roars as you reach its floor and before its biggest blow, and has its own warning, cry and death (the Gryphon's screech, the Kraken's deep wet roar, the Wyvern's reptile call, the Manticore's lion roar, the Firebird's scream, the Chimera's lion, goat and serpent at once). The train rumbles and its wheels clack over the rail joints, the whistle blows as you depart, the cars' doors slide as you walk through and the outfitter's bell rings; crystals clink when you buy and sell, the workshop bubbles as you brew and rings with hammer on anvil as you craft. Items sound like themselves when used (a drink, a puff of smoke, a vial, oil, a ward, a flask that shatters, a flare that catches). Puzzles have their own sounds (mirrors creak as they turn, ice scrapes and bumps, statues murmur as they speak, the lock's key turns, eyes open, plates slide, levers clank, pedestals light, a chime when solved and a groan of stone on a failure), as do the Makers (meetings, glimpses, fragments, a grant), the secret essences, Life's mending and Unmaking, the vessel filling, the Regard, a summit won, the Proving's shadow, the rift and the Entity's tips (a soft chime). The bond spells and the Unbound's forms and effects sound like their family: lightning and thunder, wind, water and waves, frost, fire and fire blasts, stone and sand, iron, blades, poison, life, runes and Unmaking. Each tower has its own ambience under the music (wind in the Gryphon's halls, dripping water in the Kraken's, the forge's boiler and fire in the Wyvern's, a dry wind and trickling sand in the Manticore's, a high wind and embers in the Firebird's, a low wind, birds and growls in the Thornwild), and so does the train; one music track plays under all of them.
- **Three telegraphed monsters.** A slime that hops at you, an archer that locks its aim before firing (a red line), and a knight that winds up a lunge (a filling red bar). Parry the knight's lunge to stagger it.

## What to look for when testing

- Does the base climber feel clumsy but fair?
- Does rolling through attacks to fill the legs feel rewarding or tedious?
- Is overdrawing tempting in a tight spot, and does the burn feel like a real cost?
- Can you read every enemy attack before it lands?
- Do Fine and Rare finds feel worth hunting, without the HUD filling up? Are the trait lines and "(capped)" clear on a phone?
- Is the item ring (hold the Item button, slide, let go) quick enough to use mid-fight, and does a tap still just use the quick item?

```bash
godot --headless -s tests/reach_sweep.gd -- 200        # floor plans: 200 per tower and floor
godot --headless -s tests/reach_sweep.gd -- live 40    # floors as the game builds them
```

The floor bot (`tests/parts/floor_bot.gd` plays four floors in the parts run, at twice the speed; its heavier sweep is its own script) plays real floors the way a player would, through the same inputs a controller gives: it walks by path, opens doors by walking up to them, fights with normal attacks, does the floor's task (strikes the levers, carries the orb, walks the runes, solves the puzzle with what its generator knows: the true plate, the mirrors' turns, the path over the stones, the slides on the ice, the pushes for a block) and walks up the stair. On the ice it plays one slide at a time with the puzzle's own solver: it clears the monsters about first, steps on from a way on it picked (lined up on the tile's middle, standing still), waits until each slide has fully stopped, and plans the next from wherever it ended up, so a blow or a monster in the lane costs it nothing; walking round the ice it never cuts a corner onto it. A monster that stays out on the ice it goes after down a lane the monster stands in. `tests/parts/ice_hall.gd` checks the slide graph over a few hundred generated halls, the real controls in a real hall, and the bot on a few Ice Halls. It's given a sturdy climber (1500 health, and the dev tool's No dying: it counts the close calls instead) so the sweep tests the floors rather than the fighting. It reports how many floors it finished, the time per floor, every failure and why, and how many rooms have no payoff and dead ends hold nothing:

```bash
godot --headless --fixed-fps 60 -s tests/floor_bot_sweep.gd -- 2      # 2 floors per tower and depth (108 floors)
godot --headless --fixed-fps 60 -s tests/floor_bot_sweep.gd -- 3 kind=runes tower=kraken
godot --headless --fixed-fps 60 -s tests/floor_bot_sweep.gd -- 2 kind=ice     # only Ice Halls, every tower
```

The bot also turns every Wyvern crank it can reach while its bridge is up (its floor script's `bot_step`), keeps clear of furnaces' heat and sand pits when it can (`bot_grid`), and does the towers' own tasks (`bot_task`: strikes the bellows; turns each sun stone to its seal and waits for the sun). A tower's own task kind is only forced in its own tower. `tests/parts/towers_wyvern_manticore.gd` covers the Wyvern's and the Manticore's floors: their shapes, their systems, their tasks, creatures and scenarios, reachability over thirty floors of each, and the bot doing their tasks.

## Rebuilding the sounds

The finished sounds are committed, so you only need this to change them. Each game sound is defined in `SOUNDS` at the top of `tools/build_audio.py` (which source clips to layer, how loud, and an optional low-pass/high-pass to tame harsh highs or rumble). Needs Python 3 with numpy, ffmpeg, and to fetch the sources `7z` or `pip install py7zr` (one pack comes as .7z). The ambience loops for each tower and the train are defined in `AMBIENCE` just below (long beds, and one-shots scattered at random: drips, distant cries, an anvil). The effects are imported with QOA compression (the build tool writes that into each new sound's `.import` file), which keeps the web build small. The smoke test checks that every sound the code plays is in the bank, that every monster has its hurt cry and death and every beast its roar and warning, and that every tower has its ambience.

```bash
tools/fetch_audio_sources.sh
```

```bash
python3 tools/build_audio.py
```
